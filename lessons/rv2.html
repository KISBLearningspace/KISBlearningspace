Grade 10 book: Revision Topic 2 “Percentage and Speed”. Related lessons: Ratio, Rate and Proportion; Fractions.

Guiding questions

  • How do we find a percentage of an amount and a percentage change?
  • Why can a 20% rise followed by a 20% fall not return to the start?
  • How are speed, distance and time linked, and how do we find average speed?

Learning outcomes

  • Find a percentage of an amount and express one quantity as a percentage of another.
  • Calculate percentage increase, decrease and the original amount (reverse percentage).
  • Use multipliers for repeated change.
  • Use speed = ⟦distance|time⟧ and its rearrangements.
  • Find average speed for a whole journey.

2Study Notes

Percentage of an amount. p% of A = ⟦p|100⟧ × A. 15% of 80 = 0.15 × 80 = 12.
One quantity as a percentage. ⟦part|whole⟧ × 100%. 18 out of 24 is ⟦18|24⟧ × 100 = 75%.
Multipliers. Increase by p%: multiply by 1 + ⟦p|100⟧. Decrease by p%: multiply by 1 − ⟦p|100⟧. Increase 12% → × 1.12. Decrease 8% → × 0.92.
Percentage change. ⟦new − original|original⟧ × 100%.
Reverse percentage. Original = new ÷ multiplier. After a 20% rise to $60, the original is 60 ÷ 1.2 = $50.
Repeated change. Multiply the multipliers: a 20% rise then a 20% fall gives × 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss overall.
Speed. speed = ⟦distance|time⟧, distance = speed × time, time = ⟦distance|speed⟧. Average speed = ⟦total distance|total time⟧ (not the average of two speeds).

Example 1 (percentage of amount). Find 35% of $240.
  1. Convert. 35% = 0.35
  2. Multiply. 0.35 × 240 = 84
Answer: $84
Example 2 (percentage change). A phone costs $400 and is sold for $340. Find the percentage decrease.
  1. Change. 400 − 340 = 60
  2. Divide by original. ⟦60|400⟧ = 0.15
Answer: 15% decrease
Example 3 (multiplier). Increase $85 by 12%.
  1. Multiplier. 1.12
  2. Calculate. 85 × 1.12 = 95.20
Answer: $95.20
Example 4 (reverse percentage). After a 25% discount a jacket costs $90. Find the original price.
  1. Multiplier. 100% − 25% = 75% = 0.75
  2. Original = new ÷ multiplier. 90 ÷ 0.75 = 120
Answer: $120
Example 5 (repeated change). A car worth $20 000 loses 15% in year 1 and 10% in year 2. Find the value after 2 years.
  1. Multipliers. 0.85 and 0.90
  2. Calculate. 20 000 × 0.85 × 0.90 = 15 300
Answer: $15 300
Example 6 (speed). A cyclist rides 45 km in 2.5 hours. Find the speed.
  1. Use speed = ⟦distance|time⟧. ⟦45|2.5⟧ = 18
Answer: 18 km/h
Example 7 (time). How long does a 270 km trip take at 90 km/h? Give the answer in hours and minutes.
  1. time = ⟦distance|speed⟧. ⟦270|90⟧ = 3 hours
Answer: 3 hours
Example 8 (average speed). A car travels 60 km at 40 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h. Find the average speed.
  1. Times. ⟦60|40⟧ = 1.5 h and ⟦60|60⟧ = 1 h
  2. Totals. 120 km in 2.5 h
  3. Average. ⟦120|2.5⟧ = 48
Answer: 48 km/h (not 50)
Common mistakes.
① Dividing by the new value instead of the original in percentage change.
② Reverse percentage: multiplying by 0.75 instead of dividing.
③ Adding percentages for repeated change instead of multiplying.
④ Averaging two speeds instead of using total distance ÷ total time.
⑤ Writing 1.5 hours as 1 hour 50 minutes (1.5 h = 1 h 30 min).

3History & Facts

  • Ancient Rome: “per centum” (by the hundred) was used for taxes and interest.
  • 15th–17th century: merchants used percentages for profit, interest and exchange; the % symbol evolved from Italian “per cento”.
  • Galileo (1638): studied motion and the link between distance and time.

5Real-World Applications

  • Shopping: discounts, VAT and sales tax.
  • Banking: interest and loans.
  • Travel: journey times and average speed on road trips.
  • Sport: pace of runners and cyclists.
  • Science: percentage error in experiments.
