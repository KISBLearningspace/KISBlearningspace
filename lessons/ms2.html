Purpose: A timed practice paper for olympiad-style competitions, for Grades 7–9. All 25 questions are original. They use the tricks from the Olympiad lessons: testing options, patterns, algebra identities, number theory, counting, inequalities and geometry.
How to use
Find a quiet place, set 60 minutes, press Start timer and work without a calculator. Choose an answer for every question. Then press Check my answers. Open the solutions at the end of the page and mark which trick you could have used.

Guiding questions

  • Which questions give the most marks for the least time?
  • Which trick would have saved me time on each question I missed?
  • How can I plan my 60 minutes?

Learning outcomes

  • Practise working under time pressure.
  • Choose the best trick for each question.
  • Check answers and learn from the solutions.
  • Plan time: A about 20 minutes, B about 25 minutes, C about 15 minutes.
  • Record the trick you used for every question.

10Exam-style Questions

Answers and solutions. Open each solution after you have tried the question.

Question 1 [3 marks] Answer: (E) 81
Solution3x = 27, so x = 9 and x² = 81.
Question 2 [3 marks] Answer: (C) 18
SolutionThe total is 60. 60 − (10 + 12 + 20) = 18.
Question 3 [3 marks] Answer: (B) 10
Solution48 = 2⁴ × 3, so (4 + 1)(1 + 1) = 10.
Question 4 [3 marks] Answer: (D) $100
Solution80 is 80% of the original, so the original is 80 ÷ 0.8 = $100.
Question 5 [3 marks] Answer: (A) 80°
Solution9 parts = 180°, so 1 part = 20° and the largest is 4 × 20° = 80°.
Question 6 [3 marks] Answer: (A) 11
Solution√{49} = 7, √{81} = 9 and √{25} = 5, so 7 + 9 − 5 = 11.
Question 7 [3 marks] Answer: (D) 7
SolutionThe last digits of powers of 3 cycle 3, 9, 7, 1. 2027 = 4 × 506 + 3, so it is the 3rd in the cycle: 7.
Question 8 [3 marks] Answer: (C) 33
SolutionMultiples of 4: 25. Multiples of 6: 16. Multiples of 12: 8. So 25 + 16 − 8 = 33.
Question 9 [3 marks] Answer: (B) 8 : 15
SolutionMake b equal: a : b = 8 : 12 and b : c = 12 : 15. So a : c = 8 : 15.
Question 10 [3 marks] Answer: (E) 8
Solution(n − 2) × 180 = 1080, so n − 2 = 6 and n = 8.
Question 11 [4 marks] Answer: (D) 11
SolutionSquare: x² − 2 + ⟦1|x²⟧ = 9, so x² + ⟦1|x²⟧ = 11.
Question 12 [4 marks] Answer: (B) 48 km/h
SolutionAverage speed = ⟦2 × 40 × 60|40 + 60⟧ = 48 km/h (not 50).
Question 13 [4 marks] Answer: (E) 136
SolutionEnds in 0: 9 × 8 = 72. Ends in 5: the hundreds digit has 8 choices (not 0, not 5), the tens digit has 8 choices: 64. Total 136.
Question 14 [4 marks] Answer: (C) 7.5 hours
Solution⟦1|12⟧ + ⟦1|20⟧ = ⟦8|60⟧ = ⟦2|15⟧ per hour, so 7.5 hours.
Question 15 [4 marks] Answer: (A) −4%
Solution1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, so the area decreases by 4%.
Question 16 [4 marks] Answer: (C) ⟦29|30⟧
SolutionEach term is ⟦1|n⟧ − ⟦1|n + 1⟧, so the sum is 1 − ⟦1|30⟧ = ⟦29|30⟧.
Question 17 [4 marks] Answer: (A) 4
Solution2³ = 8 leaves remainder 1. 50 = 3 × 16 + 2, so 2⁵⁰ leaves the same remainder as 2² = 4.
Question 18 [4 marks] Answer: (D) 13
Solution36 − 10 = 26 and 26 ÷ 2 = 13.
Question 19 [4 marks] Answer: (B) 2
Solution(a − b)(a + b) = 33 = 1 × 33 or 3 × 11. These give (a, b) = (17, 16) and (7, 4).
Question 20 [4 marks] Answer: (E) 480
SolutionTotal 6! = 720. Together: 2 × 5! = 240. Not together: 720 − 240 = 480.
Question 21 [6 marks] Answer: 930
SolutionThis is the sum of the even numbers 2 + 4 + … + 60 = 2 × (1 + 2 + … + 30) = 2 × 465 = 930. (Check: 1830 − 900 = 930.)
Question 22 [6 marks] Answer: 24
Solution(x + 1)(y + 1) = 48. Factor pairs with both factors at least 2: (2, 24), (3, 16), (4, 12), (6, 8) and reverses. The sums x + y are 24, 17, 14, 12. The largest is 24.
Question 23 [6 marks] Answer: 120
Solution10a + b = 4(a + b) gives 6a = 3b, so b = 2a. The numbers are 12, 24, 36, 48. Sum = 120.
Question 24 [6 marks] Answer: 40
SolutionBoys 3k and girls 2k. 3k − 4 = 2k + 4 gives k = 8. The class had 5k = 40 students.
Question 25 [6 marks] Answer: 3
SolutionThe smallest is 17 (17 = 5 × 3 + 2 = 7 × 2 + 3). The pattern repeats every 35: 17, 52, 87. So 3 numbers.
