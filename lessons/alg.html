Syllabus: Prior learning for IB Mathematics: analysis and approaches (algebra skills used in SL 1.x and throughout the course). This lesson starts from the very beginning: what letters mean in maths, and then how to add, subtract, multiply and divide algebraic terms in small steps.
Textbook references (sections only, no copied text)
Grade 10 book: Revision Topic 3 “Algebraic Manipulation and Formulae” (pp. 76–81) and Revision Practice 3 (p. 79), question 1 (simplify, expand, substitute). That section is revision for older students, so use this lesson first.

Guiding questions

  • Why do we use letters in maths, and what does a letter stand for?
  • Which terms can be added together and which cannot?
  • How is multiplying algebraic terms different from adding them?

Learning outcomes

  • Use the words variable, constant, coefficient, term, expression and like terms correctly.
  • Substitute numbers into an expression, including negative numbers.
  • Add and subtract like terms, including subtracting a bracket.
  • Multiply and divide algebraic terms using the laws of indices.
  • Expand a single bracket and simplify, and check the answer by substitution.

2Study Notes

Variable. A letter (such as x or a) that stands for a number that can change or that we do not know yet. Constant: a number on its own, such as 7. Coefficient: the number multiplied by a variable. In 5x the coefficient is 5. Term: one part of an expression, with its sign: in 5x² − 3x + 7 the terms are 5x², −3x and +7. Expression: terms joined by + or −. (An equation has an equals sign; an expression does not.)
Writing algebra. We leave out the multiplication sign: 3x means 3 × x, ab means a × b, and x² means x × x. Also ⟦x|2⟧ means x ÷ 2. Also x means 1x (the coefficient is 1), and −x means −1x.
Like terms have exactly the same letters with the same powers: 3x and 5x are like terms; 2a² and −7a² are like terms. Unlike terms are different: 3x and 3x², or 2a and 2b. Like terms can be added or subtracted. Unlike terms cannot.
The rules in short
① Add / subtract: only like terms. Add or subtract the coefficients and keep the letters: 3x + 5x = 8x.
② Multiply: multiply the coefficients, and add the powers of the same letter: 3x² × 2x³ = 6x⁵.
③ Divide: divide the coefficients, and subtract the powers: ⟦6x⁵|2x²⟧ = 3x³.
④ Brackets: multiply everything inside by the number outside. A minus sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of every term inside.
Think of it as…Example
3x + 5x: 3 apples + 5 apples8x (8 apples)
3x + 2y: 3 apples + 2 bananascannot combine (3 apples and 2 bananas)
x × x = x²the “area” of a square of side x
2(x + 3): 2 groups of (x + 3)2x + 6

Example 1 (the parts of an expression). In the expression 5x² − 3x + 7, name the terms, the coefficients and the constant.
  1. Split into terms at each + or − sign (keep the sign with the term). 5x², −3x, +7. There are 3 terms.
  2. Find the coefficients (the number in front of the letters). 5 in 5x² and −3 in −3x.
  3. Find the constant (the term with no letter). 7.
Answer: Terms: 5x², −3x, 7; coefficients 5 and −3; constant 7.
Example 2 (substitution). Find the value of (a) 3x + 2 when x = 4, (b) 3x + 2 when x = −2, (c) x² − 5 when x = −3.
  1. (a) Replace x by 4 (write 3x as 3 × 4). 3 × 4 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14.
  2. (b) Replace x by −2. Use brackets for a negative number. 3 × (−2) + 2 = −6 + 2 = −4.
  3. (c) Replace x by −3, in brackets. (−3)² − 5.
  4. (c) Square first: a negative times a negative is positive. (−3)² = 9, so 9 − 5 = 4.
Answer: (a) 14; (b) −4; (c) 4.
Example 3 (add like terms). Simplify (a) 3x + 5x, (b) 7a + 2b − 3a + b, (c) 4x² + 3x − x² + 2x.
  1. (a) Same letter, so add the coefficients. 3 + 5 = 8, so 8x.
  2. (b) Group the a terms and the b terms (keep the signs). (7a − 3a) + (2b + b).
  3. (b) Combine each group. 7a − 3a = 4a and 2b + b = 3b (b means 1b). So 4a + 3b.
  4. (c) Group x² terms and x terms. (4x² − x²) + (3x + 2x) = 3x² + 5x.
  5. (c) x² and x are unlike, so we stop. The answer is 3x² + 5x.
Answer: (a) 8x; (b) 4a + 3b; (c) 3x² + 5x.
Example 4 (subtract, including brackets). Simplify (a) (5x + 3) − (2x + 1) and (b) (7a + 2) − (3a − 4).
  1. (a) Remove the brackets. The minus sign changes the sign of every term in the second bracket. 5x + 3 − 2x − 1.
  2. (a) Group like terms. (5x − 2x) + (3 − 1) = 3x + 2.
  3. (b) Remove the brackets: −(3a − 4) becomes −3a + 4. 7a + 2 − 3a + 4.
  4. (b) Group like terms. (7a − 3a) + (2 + 4) = 4a + 6.
  5. Check (b) with a = 1. Original: (7 + 2) − (3 − 4) = 9 − (−1) = 10. Answer: 4 + 6 = 10 ✓.
Answer: (a) 3x + 2; (b) 4a + 6.
Example 5 (unlike terms cannot be combined). Simplify (a) 3x + 2y and (b) 3x + 5 − x + 2.
  1. (a) x and y are different letters. 3x and 2y are unlike terms, so 3x + 2y is already simplified. (It is not 5xy and not 5x.)
  2. (b) Group the x terms and the numbers. (3x − x) + (5 + 2).
  3. (b) Combine. 3x − x = 2x (x means 1x), and 5 + 2 = 7. So 2x + 7.
Answer: (a) 3x + 2y (cannot simplify); (b) 2x + 7.
Example 6 (multiply terms). Simplify (a) 3x × 4, (b) 2a × 5b, (c) x × x, (d) 3x² × 2x³, (e) −4m × 3m.
  1. Rule. Multiply the numbers (coefficients) first, then the letters. For the same letter, add the powers.
  2. (a) 3 × 4 = 12, so 12x.
  3. (b) 2 × 5 = 10, and a × b = ab, so 10ab.
  4. (c) x × x = x² (x¹ × x¹ = x1+1).
  5. (d) 3 × 2 = 6 and x² × x³ = x2+3 = x⁵, so 6x⁵.
  6. (e) Negative times positive is negative: −4 × 3 = −12, and m × m = m², so −12m².
Answer: (a) 12x; (b) 10ab; (c) x²; (d) 6x⁵; (e) −12m².
Example 7 (divide terms). Simplify (a) 12x ÷ 4, (b) ⟦15a²b|5a⟧, (c) ⟦6x²|3x⟧, (d) ⟦8x³|4x⟧.
  1. Rule. Divide the numbers, then divide the letters by cancelling (subtract the powers).
  2. (a) 12 ÷ 4 = 3, so 3x.
  3. (b) Numbers. 15 ÷ 5 = 3.
  4. (b) Letters: a² ÷ a leaves a, and b has nothing to cancel with. So 3ab.
  5. (c) 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and x² ÷ x = x, so 2x.
  6. (d) 8 ÷ 4 = 2 and x³ ÷ x = x3−1 = x², so 2x².
Answer: (a) 3x; (b) 3ab; (c) 2x; (d) 2x².
Example 8 (expand a bracket). Expand (a) 3(x + 4), (b) −2(x − 5), (c) x(x + 3).
  1. Rule. Multiply every term inside the bracket by the term outside. Keep track of the signs.
  2. (a) 3 × x = 3x and 3 × 4 = 12, so 3x + 12.
  3. (b) −2 × x = −2x and −2 × (−5) = +10 (negative times negative is positive), so −2x + 10.
  4. (c) x × x = x² and x × 3 = 3x, so x² + 3x.
  5. Check (a) with x = 2. 3(2 + 4) = 18 and 3(2) + 12 = 18 ✓.
Answer: (a) 3x + 12; (b) −2x + 10; (c) x² + 3x.
Example 9 (expand and simplify). Simplify 5(2x − 1) − 3(x + 2).
  1. Expand the first bracket. 5(2x − 1) = 10x − 5.
  2. Expand the second bracket, with its minus sign. −3(x + 2) = −3x − 6.
  3. Write everything together. 10x − 5 − 3x − 6.
  4. Group like terms. (10x − 3x) + (−5 − 6) = 7x − 11.
  5. Check with x = 1. Original: 5(1) − 3(3) = 5 − 9 = −4. Answer: 7 − 11 = −4 ✓.
Answer: 7x − 11
Example 10 (algebra in a problem). A rectangle has sides x cm and (x + 3) cm. Write the perimeter in its simplest form, and find it when x = 5.
  1. Perimeter = the sum of all four sides. x + (x + 3) + x + (x + 3).
  2. Group the x terms and the numbers. (x + x + x + x) + (3 + 3).
  3. Combine. 4x + 6.
  4. Substitute x = 5. 4 × 5 + 6 = 26.
  5. Check with the sides 5 and 8. 5 + 8 + 5 + 8 = 26 ✓.
Answer: Perimeter = 4x + 6 cm; 26 cm when x = 5.
Common mistakes.
① Adding unlike terms: 3x + 2y is not 5xy, and 3x + 3x² is not 6x³.
② Writing 3x × 2x = 6x instead of 6x² (the powers add).
③ Forgetting that a minus in front of a bracket changes every sign inside.
④ Forgetting that x means 1x, so x + x = 2x (not x²).
⑤ Squaring a negative number without brackets: (−3)² = 9, but −3² = −9.
A good habit: test your answer by putting in a number such as x = 2 or x = 1.

3History & Facts

  • Al-Khwarizmi (about 820 CE): his book on “al-jabr” (balancing) gave us the word algebra. He used words, not letters.
  • François Viète (1590s): started using letters for unknown and known numbers in a systematic way.
  • René Descartes (1637): introduced using the last letters x, y, z for unknowns and the first letters a, b, c for known values, and the small raised numbers for powers like x².
  • Fact: the “=” sign was invented by Robert Recorde in 1557, because “no two things can be more equal” than two parallel lines.

5Real-World Applications

  • Phone and taxi bills: cost = 40 + 12d, where d is the distance. The letter d stands for any distance.
  • Spreadsheets: a formula such as =2*A1 + B1 is algebra with cell names as letters.
  • Science: speed = distance ÷ time is a formula with letters v = ⟦d|t⟧.
  • Games and coding: a score, a position or a level is stored in a variable.
  • Money: total = price × number of items + delivery.
