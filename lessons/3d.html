Example 1 (cuboid). A cuboid is 6 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. Find the space diagonal and the angle between it and the base.
  1. Space diagonal. d = √{6² + 4² + 3²} = √{36 + 16 + 9} = √{61} ≈ 7.81 cm.
  2. Draw the right-angled triangle. The diagonal is the hypotenuse. The side opposite θ is the height 3, so sin θ = ⟦3|√{61}⟧.
  3. Find the angle. θ = sin−1(⟦3|√{61}⟧) ≈ 22.6°.
Answer: Diagonal ≈ 7.81 cm; θ ≈ 22.6°.
Angle between a line and a plane. Drop a perpendicular from the line to the plane. The angle is between the line and its projection on the plane. Always draw the right-angled triangle formed by the line, its projection and the perpendicular.
Example 2 (pyramid). A right pyramid has a square base of side 6 and vertical height 4. Find the volume, surface area, and the angles of an edge and a face with the base.
  1. Volume = ⟦1|3⟧ × base area × height. ⟦1|3⟧ × 36 × 4 = 48.
  2. Slant height of a face. It forms a right triangle with the height 4 and half the side 3: l = √{4² + 3²} = 5.
  3. Surface area. Base 36 + four triangles: 4 × (⟦1|2⟧ × 6 × 5) = 60. Total 96.
  4. Edge and base. The half-diagonal of the base is 3√{2} ≈ 4.243, so tan θ = ⟦4|3√{2}⟧ and θ ≈ 43.3°.
  5. Face and base. Use the half-side 3: tan φ = ⟦4|3⟧, so φ ≈ 53.1°.
Answer: V = 48; surface area = 96; edge angle ≈ 43.3°; face angle ≈ 53.1°.
Example 3 (cone and sphere). A cone has r = 3 and h = 4. A sphere has r = 5. Find the volumes and the cone’s curved area and the sphere’s surface area.
  1. Cone slant height. l = √{3² + 4²} = 5.
  2. Cone volume. V = ⟦1|3⟧πr²h = ⟦1|3⟧π(9)(4) = 12π ≈ 37.7.
  3. Cone curved area. πrl = π(3)(5) = 15π ≈ 47.1.
  4. Sphere volume. V = ⟦4|3⟧πr³ = ⟦4|3⟧π(125) = ⟦500π|3⟧ ≈ 523.6.
  5. Sphere surface area. 4πr² = 4π(25) = 100π ≈ 314.2.
Answer: Cone: V ≈ 37.7, curved area ≈ 47.1. Sphere: V ≈ 523.6, area ≈ 314.2.
Example 4 (composite). An ice-cream is a cone (r = 3, h = 8) with a hemisphere of the same radius on top. Find the volume and the outer surface area.
  1. Cone volume. ⟦1|3⟧π(9)(8) = 24π.
  2. Hemisphere volume (half a sphere). ⟦1|2⟧ × ⟦4|3⟧π(27) = ⟦2|3⟧π(27) = 18π.
  3. Total volume. 24π + 18π = 42π ≈ 131.9.
  4. Cone slant height. l = √{3² + 8²} = √{73}. Curved area = π(3)√{73} ≈ 80.5.
  5. Hemisphere curved area. 2πr² = 2π(9) = 18π ≈ 56.5.
  6. Add (the flat faces are joined, so they are not counted). 80.5 + 56.5 ≈ 137.1.
Answer: Volume 42π ≈ 131.9; surface area ≈ 137.1.
Example 5 (angle between two lines). In a cube of side 2, find the angle between the face diagonal AC (on the base) and the space diagonal AG.
  1. Find a right-angled triangle. Triangle ACG has a right angle at C, because CG is vertical and AC is on the base.
  2. Find the sides. AC = √{2² + 2²} = 2√{2} and CG = 2.
  3. Use tan at A. tan θ = ⟦CG|AC⟧ = ⟦2|2√{2}⟧ = ⟦1|√{2}⟧.
  4. Find the angle. θ = tan−1(⟦1|√{2}⟧) ≈ 35.3°.
Answer: θ ≈ 35.3°

3History & Facts

  • Archimedes (about 250 BCE): proved that a sphere has two thirds of the volume of its enclosing cylinder, and asked for this diagram to be carved on his tomb.
  • Egyptian builders (about 2500 BCE): the Great Pyramid of Giza has a square base of about 230 m and an original height of about 147 m. Egyptian scribes knew how to find the volume of a truncated pyramid.
  • Euclid’s Elements (about 300 BCE): Book XI deals with solid geometry, including the angle between lines and planes.
  • Fact: a cone has one third the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height, and a pyramid one third of the matching prism.

5Real-World Applications

  • Architecture: pyramids, cones and domes: volume decides how much material is needed, surface area decides cladding or paint.
  • Packaging and shipping: the longest item that fits in a box is its space diagonal.
  • Construction and drones: angles between a line and the ground are slopes of ramps, roofs and cable stays.
  • Food and manufacturing: ice-cream cones, silos and tanks are composite solids made from cones, cylinders and hemispheres.
  • Astronomy: planets are close to spheres, so volume and surface area follow from the radius.

6IB Links: TOK, International-mindedness & ATL

  • TOK: Archimedes found the sphere’s volume by “weighing” slices in his mind. Is imagining a proof the same as doing one?
  • International-mindedness: Egyptian and Maya pyramids and Thai chedis (stupas) use the same square-based shapes. How do cultures use geometry in sacred architecture?

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. A cuboid is 5 cm by 12 cm by 9 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal to 3 s.f.
    Answer
    d = √{25 + 144 + 81}
    = √{250} ≈ 15.8 cm
  2. A cone has radius 5 cm and vertical height 12 cm. Find the slant height, the volume and the curved surface area.
    Answer
    l = 13 cm
    V = ⟦1|3⟧π(25)(12) = 100π ≈ 314 cm³
    Curved area = π(5)(13) = 65π ≈ 204 cm²
  3. Find the volume and surface area of a sphere of radius 3 cm, in terms of π.
    Answer
    V = ⟦4|3⟧π(27) = 36π cm³
    Surface area = 4π(9) = 36π cm²
  4. A right pyramid has a square base of side 8 and vertical height 3. Find the volume and the slant height of a face.
    Answer
    V = ⟦1|3⟧(64)(3)
    = 64
    Slant height = √{9 + 16}
    = 5

Homework

  1. In the pyramid in CW 4, find the angle between a sloping edge and the base.
    Answer
    Half-diagonal = 4√{2} ≈ 5.657
    tan θ = ⟦3|5.657⟧
    θ ≈ 28.0°
  2. A cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 8, BC = 6 and AE = 5. Find AG and the angle between AG and the base ABCD.
    Answer
    AC = 10
    AG = √{125} ≈ 11.2
    tan θ = ⟦5|10⟧
    θ ≈ 26.6°
  3. A cylinder of radius 2 m and height 5 m is topped by a hemisphere of radius 2 m. Find the total volume.
    Answer
    V = π(4)(5) + ⟦2|3⟧π(8)
    = 20π + ⟦16π|3⟧
    = ⟦76π|3⟧ ≈ 79.6 m³
  4. A sphere has surface area 100π. Find its radius and volume.
    Answer
    4πr² = 100π
    so r = 5
    V = ⟦4|3⟧π(125) ≈ 524
