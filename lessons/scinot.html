1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: Mathematics: analysis and approaches · Topic 1 Number and algebra · SL 1.1 (operations with numbers in the form a × 10k where 1 ≤ a < 10 and k is an integer). HL students study the same content.

Guiding questions

  • Why do scientists write very large and very small numbers in standard form?
  • How does the power of 10 show the size of a number?
  • How can calculations be done without changing the form?

Learning outcomes

  • Write numbers in the form a × 10k with 1 ≤ a < 10.
  • Convert back to ordinary numbers.
  • Compare numbers in standard form.
  • Multiply, divide, add and subtract in standard form.

2Study Notes

A number is in standard form when written as
a × 10k, 1 ≤ |a| < 10, k ∈ ℤ
NumberStandard form
30003 × 103
45 0004.5 × 104
0.00323.2 × 10−3
−5 600 000−5.6 × 106
0.181.8 × 10−1
  • Large numbers: move the decimal point left; k is positive.
  • Small numbers: move the decimal point right; k is negative.
Example 1. Calculate, giving each answer in the form a × 10k with 1 ≤ a < 10: (a) (2 × 103)(5 × 104), (b) ⟦6 × 105|3 × 102⟧, (c) (4.2 × 103) + (3.1 × 103).
  1. (a) Multiply the numbers, then the powers of 10. 2 × 5 = 10 and 103 × 104 = 107 (add the powers). So the product is 10 × 107.
  2. (a) Put it in standard form. 10 is not between 1 and 10, so write 10 = 1 × 101: 10 × 107 = 1 × 108.
  3. (b) Divide the numbers, then the powers. 6 ÷ 3 = 2 and 105 ÷ 102 = 103 (subtract the powers). The result 2 × 103 is already in standard form.
  4. (c) Same power of 10, so add the front numbers. 4.2 + 3.1 = 7.3, so the sum is 7.3 × 103. (If the powers were different, first rewrite one number so both use the same power.)
Answer: (a) 1 × 108 (b) 2 × 103 (c) 7.3 × 103
Example 2. Which is larger, 3.2 × 105 or 7.1 × 104?
  1. Compare the powers of 10 first. Both numbers are in standard form, so the larger power of 10 decides: 5 > 4.
  2. Check by writing them out. 3.2 × 105 = 320 000 and 7.1 × 104 = 71 000.
Answer: 3.2 × 105 is larger (the front numbers matter only when the powers are equal).

Technology: a GDC shows 3.2 × 105 as 3.2E5. In your written answer always write 3.2 × 105.

Think: Why must a be at least 1 but less than 10? Is 12 × 103 in standard form?

3History & Facts

  • Archimedes (about 250 BCE): in The Sand Reckoner he built a system to name numbers larger than the grains of sand in the universe.
  • Today: calculators and computers use the same idea, displaying numbers as 3.2E5.
  • Fact: a light-year is about 9.46 × 1015 m.

5Real-World Applications

  • Speed of light: 3.0 × 108 m s−1
  • Mass of an electron: 9.1 × 10−31 kg
  • Distance from Earth to the Sun: 1.5 × 1011 m
  • Size of a bacterium: 2 × 10−6 m

  • International-mindedness: scientists worldwide share standard form as a common language.

    Answer7 × 10−5.

    Answer2 × 103.
  3. Calculate (3 × 10−2)(4 × 105).
    Answer12 × 103 = 1.2 × 104.
  4. Light takes how long to travel 1.5 × 1011 m at 3.0 × 108 m s−1?
    Answer⟦1.5 × 1011|3.0 × 108⟧ = 0.5 × 103 = 5 × 102 s.
