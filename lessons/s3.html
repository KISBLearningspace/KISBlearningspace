Guiding questions

  • What single number best describes a typical value of a data set?
  • Why does one very large value change the mean but not the median?
  • How can we estimate an average when the data are only given in classes?

Learning outcomes

  • Define and calculate the mean, median and mode from raw data and frequency tables.
  • Estimate the mean, and find the modal class and median class, for grouped data.
  • Choose the most suitable average for a given situation.
  • Describe the effect of an outlier and of adding to or multiplying every value by a constant.

2Study Notes

Mean. The sum of all the values divided by the number of values:
x̄ = ⟦Σx|n⟧
For a frequency table:
x̄ = ⟦Σfx|Σf⟧   where n = Σf
For grouped data use the midpoint of each class as x. The result is only an estimate.
Median. The middle value when the data are in order. For n values it is the value at position ⟦n + 1|2⟧: if n is odd it is the middle value, if n is even it is the mean of the two middle values.
Mode. The value that occurs most often. A data set may have no mode, one mode (unimodal), or two modes (bimodal). For grouped data the modal class is the class with the highest frequency (equal class widths). Mode is the only average for qualitative data.
AverageAdvantageDisadvantage
MeanUses every value; used in further calculationsAffected by outliers and skew
MedianNot affected by outliers; good for skewed dataIgnores the size of most values
ModeWorks for categories; easy to seeMay not exist or may not be unique
Example 1 (raw data). Find the mean, median and mode of 4, 7, 7, 9, 12, 15, 16.
  1. Mean = sum ÷ number of values. 4 + 7 + 7 + 9 + 12 + 15 + 16 = 70, and there are 7 values, so mean = ⟦70|7⟧ = 10.
  2. Median: the middle value of ordered data. n = 7, so the 4th value, which is 9.
  3. Mode: the most frequent value. 7 appears twice.
Answer: Mean 10; median 9; mode 7.
Example 2 (frequency table). Goals scored per match in 20 matches:
Goals x01234
Frequency f35831
fx051694
  1. Mean = ⟦Σfx|Σf⟧. Σfx = 0 + 5 + 16 + 9 + 4 = 34 and Σf = 20, so mean = ⟦34|20⟧ = 1.7.
  2. Median: use cumulative frequencies. 3, 8, 16, 19, 20. The 10th and 11th values both fall where the cumulative frequency reaches 16, that is, at 2 goals.
  3. Mode. The highest frequency is 8, at 2 goals.
Answer: Mean 1.7; median 2; mode 2.
Example 3 (grouped data). Times (min) for 30 students:
Class0–1010–2020–3030–40
Frequency49125
Midpoint x5152535
f × x20135300175
  1. Use the midpoint of each class as its value. The row f × x is already shown: 20, 135, 300, 175.
  2. Estimated mean = ⟦Σfx|Σf⟧. Σfx = 20 + 135 + 300 + 175 = 630 and Σf = 30, so mean = ⟦630|30⟧ = 21 min (an estimate, since we do not know the exact values).
  3. Modal class. The highest frequency is 12, in the class 20–30.
  4. Median class. Cumulative frequencies: 4, 13, 25, 30. The 15th and 16th values lie in the class 20–30.
Answer: Estimated mean 21 min; modal class 20–30; median class 20–30.
Example 4 (combining groups). Class A has 20 students with mean 62. Class B has 30 students with mean 70. Find the combined mean.
  1. Find the total of each class (total = mean × number). A: 20 × 62 = 1240. B: 30 × 70 = 2100.
  2. Add the totals. 1240 + 2100 = 3340.
  3. Divide by the total number of students. ⟦3340|50⟧ = 66.8.
Answer: 66.8 (do not average 62 and 70: the groups have different sizes).
Example 5 (outlier). Monthly pay in thousands of baht: 30, 32, 35, 38, 40. A manager with pay 200 joins. Find the mean and median before and after.
  1. Before. Mean = ⟦175|5⟧ = 35. Median = 35 (the middle value).
  2. After: six values. Total = 175 + 200 = 375, so mean = ⟦375|6⟧ = 62.5.
  3. Median after: average of the 3rd and 4th values. ⟦35 + 38|2⟧ = 36.5.
  4. Compare. The mean rose from 35 to 62.5, but the median moved only from 35 to 36.5.
Answer: The median describes a typical worker better when there are outliers.
Example 6 (finding a missing value). The mean of five numbers is 8. Four of them are 6, 7, 9 and 10. Find the fifth.
  1. Total = mean × number. 5 × 8 = 40.
  2. Add the known numbers. 6 + 7 + 9 + 10 = 32.
  3. Subtract. The fifth number is 40 − 32 = 8.
Answer: 8
Effect of constant changes. If every value is changed to ax + b, then the new mean is a × (old mean) + b, and the new median is a × (old median) + b. The mode changes in the same way. Example: mean 12 and median 11 become mean 3(12) + 2 = 38 and median 3(11) + 2 = 35 under x → 3x + 2.
Think: ① When would the mean be a misleading “average” for house prices? ② A frequency table has two classes with the same highest frequency. What can you say about the modal class? ③ Why does the grouped mean only give an estimate?

3History & Facts

  • Ancient Greeks: used the arithmetic mean to describe averages such as the middle of two numbers; the idea of the mean for a set of measurements appeared in astronomy in the 1600s.
  • Francis Galton (1880s): popularised the median and quartiles to summarise data where outliers are common.
  • Adolphe Quetelet (1830s): introduced the idea of the “average man” using the mean of human measurements.
  • Fact: reports of “average salary” often use the median, because a few very high salaries raise the mean a lot.

5Real-World Applications

  • Economics: median household income and mean wage figures describe different things.
  • Sport: a batting average or average score per match is a mean.
  • Weather: mean monthly rainfall and median temperature.
  • Retail: the mode of shoe sizes decides which sizes to stock most.
  • Education: mean exam score of a class, and combined mean of several classes.

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. Find the mean, median and mode of: 6, 8, 8, 9, 11, 14.
    AnswerMean = ⟦56|6⟧ ≈ 9.33. Median = ⟦8 + 9|2⟧ = 8.5. Mode = 8.
  2. The mean of 6 numbers is 9. A 7th number is added and the mean becomes 10. Find the 7th number.
    AnswerOld sum = 54, new sum = 70, so the 7th number is 16.
  3. A frequency table: x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 with f = 2, 5, k, 4, 1. The mean is 2.8. Find k.
    Answer⟦33 + 3k|12 + k⟧ = 2.8, so 33 + 3k = 33.6 + 2.8k, 0.2k = 0.6, k = 3.
  4. Estimate the mean mark: 0–20: 3, 20–40: 7, 40–60: 12, 60–80: 6, 80–100: 2.
    AnswerMidpoints 10, 30, 50, 70, 90. Σfx = 30 + 210 + 600 + 420 + 180 = 1440. Mean = ⟦1440|30⟧ = 48.

Homework

  1. Salaries (thousand baht): 18, 20, 21, 22, 22, 24, 25, 26, 28, 30, 95. Find the mean and the median. Which is better as the “typical” salary?
    AnswerMean = ⟦331|11⟧ ≈ 30.1. Median = 24. The median is better because 95 is an outlier.
  2. Every salary in HW 1 rises by 10% and then 2 (thousand) is added. Find the new mean and median.
    AnswerNew mean = 1.1(30.09) + 2 ≈ 35.1. New median = 1.1(24) + 2 = 28.4.
  3. The mean of 10 numbers is 15 and the mean of 15 other numbers is 20. Find the mean of all 25 numbers.
    Answer⟦150 + 300|25⟧ = 18.
  4. Use CW 4 to find the modal class and the median class.
    AnswerModal class 40–60. Cumulative: 3, 10, 22, 28, 30; the 15th value is in 40–60.
