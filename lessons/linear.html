Linear Equations

IB Diploma Programme · Mathematics: analysis and approaches · SL 2.1, SL 2.4 · notes · lab · practice · exam-style questions · IA ideas

1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: Mathematics: analysis and approaches · Topic 2 Functions · SL 2.1 (forms of the equation of a straight line; gradient; intercepts; parallel and perpendicular lines) and SL 2.4 (finding the intersection of lines using technology). HL students study the same content.

Guiding questions

  • How does the gradient describe the rate of change between two quantities?
  • Why are there several forms of the same line, and when is each most useful?
  • How can algebra, graphs and technology be combined to find where two lines meet?

Learning outcomes

  • Solve linear equations in one variable, including brackets and fractions.
  • Find the gradient of a line from two points.
  • Write and graph a line in the form y = mx + c.
  • Recognise parallel and perpendicular lines from their gradients.
  • Solve two equations at once by substitution, elimination and graphs.
  • Model real situations such as fares, plans and conversions.

Prerequisites: operations with integers and fractions, order of operations, substitution, the coordinate plane.

2Study Notes

Linear equation in one variable
ax + b = c  (a ≠ 0)
Do the same operation to both sides until x is alone. The highest power of x is 1, so there is exactly one solution.
Example 1. Solve 3x − 7 = 11.
  1. Isolate the term with x. Add 7 to both sides: 3x = 18.
  2. Divide by the coefficient of x. x = 18 ÷ 3 = 6.
  3. Check. 3(6) − 7 = 18 − 7 = 11 ✓
Answer: x = 6
Example 2. Solve ⟦x + 3|2⟧ = 5.
  1. Clear the fraction. Multiply both sides by 2: x + 3 = 10.
  2. Isolate x. Subtract 3: x = 7.
  3. Check. ⟦7 + 3|2⟧ = 5 ✓
Answer: x = 7
Example 3 (x on both sides). Solve 4(x − 2) = 3x + 5.
  1. Expand the bracket. 4x − 8 = 3x + 5.
  2. Collect the x terms on one side. Subtract 3x: x − 8 = 5.
  3. Isolate x. Add 8: x = 13.
  4. Check. Left: 4(11) = 44. Right: 3(13) + 5 = 44 ✓
Answer: x = 13

Gradient and equation of a line

m = ⟦y₂ − y₁|x₂ − x₁⟧    y = mx + c
m is the gradient (steepness); c is the y-intercept; the x-intercept is x = ⟦−c|m⟧.
Example. Find the equation of the line through (1, 3) and (4, 9).
  1. Find the gradient. m = ⟦y₂ − y₁|x₂ − x₁⟧ = ⟦9 − 3|4 − 1⟧ = ⟦6|3⟧ = 2.
  2. Use y = mx + c with m = 2. y = 2x + c.
  3. Substitute one point to find c. Using (1, 3): 3 = 2(1) + c, so c = 1.
  4. Write the equation. y = 2x + 1.
  5. Check with the other point. (4, 9): 2(4) + 1 = 9 ✓
Answer: y = 2x + 1

Parallel lines have equal gradients. Perpendicular lines have m₁ × m₂ = −1, so the perpendicular to m = 2 has gradient −⟦1|2⟧.

Two equations at once

Example (simultaneous equations). Solve y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 7.
  1. Substitute the first equation into the second. Replace y by 2x + 1: x + (2x + 1) = 7.
  2. Solve for x. 3x + 1 = 7, so 3x = 6 and x = 2.
  3. Find y. y = 2(2) + 1 = 5.
  4. Check in the other equation. 2 + 5 = 7 ✓
Answer: The lines cross at (2, 5).
Think: ① Why does a vertical line have no gradient? ② Why can two lines have no solution together? ③ How could you check a solution without using the graph?

3Forms, Command Terms & Technology

Forms of a straight line (formula booklet)

FormEquation
Gradient-intercepty = mx + c
Generalax + by + d = 0
Point-gradienty − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Example: through (1, 3) with m = 2: y − 3 = 2(x − 1) → y = 2x + 1 → 2x − y + 1 = 0.

4History & Facts

  • Egypt (about 1650 BCE): the Rhind Papyrus shows problems solved by “false position”, a guess-and-correct method for linear equations.
  • China (about 200 BCE–50 CE): The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art solves several equations at once with a method like elimination.
  • Robert Recorde (1557): introduced the equals sign “=” using two parallel lines of equal length.
  • René Descartes (1637): linked algebra and geometry, so equations can be drawn as graphs on coordinate axes.
  • Word fact: “linear” comes from the Latin linea, meaning “line”, because the graph is a straight line.

6Real-World Applications

  • Travel: distance = speed × time is linear when speed is constant.
  • Fares and plans: a fixed fee plus a rate per kilometre or per month.
  • Temperature: F = 1.8C + 32 converts Celsius to Fahrenheit; 25 °C gives 77 °F.
  • Money: simple interest and savings plans grow by the same amount each period.
Taxi problem. The fare is 40 + 12d (baht) for d km. Find d if the fare is 148.
  1. Write the equation. 40 + 12d = 148.
  2. Subtract the fixed charge. 12d = 108.
  3. Divide. d = 108 ÷ 12 = 9.
Answer: d = 9 km

11Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style (no calculator)

Question 1 [6 marks] The line L₁ passes through A(−1, 2) and B(3, 10).
(a) Find the gradient of L₁. [2]
(b) Find the equation of L₁ in the form y = mx + c. [2]
(c) The line L₂ is perpendicular to L₁ and passes through (4, 1). Find the equation of L₂. [2]
Mark scheme(a) M1 m = ⟦10 − 2|3 − (−1)⟧; A1 m = 2.
(b) M1 y − 2 = 2(x + 1); A1 y = 2x + 4.
(c) M1 gradient = −⟦1|2⟧ and y − 1 = −⟦1|2⟧(x − 4); A1 y = −⟦1|2⟧x + 3.
Question 2 [3 marks] Show that the lines y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 7 intersect at (2, 5).
Mark schemeM1 x + (2x + 1) = 7; A1 3x = 6, x = 2; A1 y = 2(2) + 1 = 5, so the point is (2, 5) (AG).

Paper 2 style (GDC allowed)

Question 3 [6 marks] Company A charges $40 plus $12 per km. Company B charges $55 plus $9.50 per km. Let d be the distance in km.
(a) Write down an expression for the cost of each company. [2]
(b) Find the distance for which both companies charge the same, and that cost. [3]
(c) A customer travels 8 km. Which company is cheaper? [1]
Mark scheme(a) A1 CA = 40 + 12d; A1 CB = 55 + 9.5d.
(b) M1 equate (or use intersect on the GDC); A1 d = 6 km; A1 cost $112.
(c) A1 Company B: $131 compared with $136.

12Project · Plan Detectives

Driving question: Which plan is best for which customer?

Materials: two real or teacher-made price cards (for example ride fares, phone plans or gym memberships), graph paper or a spreadsheet, rulers. Groups of 3–4.

  1. Write each plan as a linear equation C = fixed fee + rate × usage.
  2. Complete a table of costs for at least 6 usage values.
  3. Graph both lines on the same axes.
  4. Find the break-even point from the graph, then confirm it by solving the two equations.
  5. Recommend a plan for three customers (light, average, heavy use) and explain why.
