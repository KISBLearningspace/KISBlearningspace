Guiding questions

  • How do we find the rule behind a list of numbers?
  • How can we add a long list of numbers without adding them one by one?
  • How do we find the 2026th item of a repeating pattern?

Learning outcomes

  • Use first and second differences to find a rule.
  • Decode “made-up operation” puzzles from examples.
  • Use pairing (Gauss) to add an arithmetic list and count its terms.
  • Use remainders to find a term in a repeating pattern or a last digit.
  • Test small cases to guess and check a formula.

2Study Notes

Trick 1: Differences. Subtract neighbouring terms. If the first differences are constant, the rule is linear. If the second differences are constant, the rule is quadratic (it contains n²).
Trick 2: Made-up operations. For a ★ b defined by examples, guess a rule of the form pa + qb + r. Use two examples to find p and q, then check with a third.
Trick 3: Pairing (Gauss). In an arithmetic list the first and last add to the same total as the second and second-last. So sum = (number of terms) × ⟦first + last|2⟧.
Counting terms: number of terms = (last − first) ÷ difference + 1.
Trick 4: Cycles. A pattern with block length k repeats. The nth item is the same as item (n mod k). If the remainder is 0, it is the last item in the block. Last digits of powers also cycle: 3, 9, 7, 1, 3, 9, 7, 1, …
Trick 5: Guess and check. Write the answers for n = 1, 2, 3. Guess a formula. Verify it for n = 4 before using it.

Example 1 [Level 1] (made-up operation). a ★ b is a rule with 3 ★ 4 = 7, 5 ★ 2 = 9 and 6 ★ 6 = 15. Find 9 ★ 7. (A) 20 (B) 21 (C) 22 (D) 23 (E) 24
  1. Guess a ★ b = 2a + b − 3. 3 ★ 4: 6 + 4 − 3 = 7 ✓; 5 ★ 2: 10 + 2 − 3 = 9 ✓; 6 ★ 6: 12 + 6 − 3 = 15 ✓
  2. Use it for 9 and 7. 18 + 7 − 3 = 22
Answer: (C) 22
Example 2 [Level 1] (pairing). Find 5 + 8 + 11 + … + 41.
  1. Count the terms: ⟦41 − 5|3⟧ + 1. 13 terms
  2. Pair first and last: 5 + 41 = 46. 13 × 46 ÷ 2 = 299
Answer: 299
Example 3 [Level 2] (cycles). The pattern R B B G repeats: R B B G R B B G … What colour is the 2026th item?
  1. Block length 4. Divide 2026 by 4. 2026 = 4 × 506 + 2
  2. The 2nd item in the block is B. Colour: B
Answer: Blue (B)
Example 4 [Level 2] (second differences). Find the 100th term of 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, …
  1. First differences: 3, 5, 7, 9. Second differences: 2, 2, 2. Quadratic: n² + something
  2. n² gives 1, 4, 9, 16, 25. Each term is 1 more. nth term = n² + 1
  3. n = 100. 10 001
Answer: 10 001
Example 5 [Level 3] (small cases). Find 1 × 2 + 2 × 3 + 3 × 4 + … + 20 × 21.
  1. Write sums for n = 1, 2, 3. 2, 8, 20
  2. Guess S(n) = ⟦n(n + 1)(n + 2)|3⟧. Check: n = 1 gives 2, n = 2 gives 8, n = 3 gives 20, n = 4: 4 × 5 × 6 ÷ 3 = 40 = 20 + 20 ✓ Use n = 20
  3. 20 × 21 × 22 ÷ 3. 3080
Answer: 3080
Example 6 [Level 3] (last digit). Find the last digit of 3^2026.
  1. Last digits of 3¹, 3², 3³, 3⁴: 3, 9, 7, 1. The cycle has length 4. 2026 = 4 × 506 + 2
  2. The 2nd digit in the cycle. 9
Answer: 9
Common mistakes.
① Counting terms as ⟦last − first|d⟧ and forgetting “+ 1”.
② Using the remainder wrongly: remainder 0 means the last item of the block, not the first.
③ Stopping after two terms and assuming the pattern.
④ Not checking a guessed rule on a third example.
⑤ Adding a list from the wrong ends when pairing.

3History & Facts

  • Carl Friedrich Gauss (about 1787): the story says that as a boy he added 1 to 100 quickly by pairing 1 + 100, 2 + 99, …
  • Fibonacci (1202): a rabbit-breeding puzzle gave the famous sequence 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, …
  • Today: pattern finding is the basis of data science and coding.

5Real-World Applications

  • Music: rhythms repeat in cycles of beats.
  • Computing: check digits and hash functions use remainders.
  • Calendar: days of the week repeat every 7 days.
  • Finance: equal savings each month form an arithmetic sum.
  • Nature: spirals and branching follow number patterns.

10Exam-style Questions

Original questions in the style of a maths olympiad. Section A = 3 marks, Section B = 4 marks, Section C = 6 marks (write the numerical answer). Open “Solution” only after trying.

Section A

Question 1 [3 marks] 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 60 = (A) 1770 (B) 1830 (C) 1860 (D) 1890 (E) 1950
Solution(B) 1830 = 60 × 61 ÷ 2.
Question 2 [3 marks] The next term of 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, … is (A) 38 (B) 40 (C) 42 (D) 44 (E) 46
Solution(C) 42. The differences are 4, 6, 8, 10, so the next difference is 12. (The terms are n(n + 1).)

Section B

Question 3 [4 marks] The digits 2, 0, 2, 6 repeat to make 2 0 2 6 2 0 2 6 …. Find the sum of the first 2026 digits. (A) 5060 (B) 5062 (C) 5064 (D) 5066 (E) 5068
Solution(B) 5062. Each block adds to 10. 2026 = 4 × 506 + 2, so there are 506 blocks (5060) plus the digits 2 and 0.

Section C

Question 4 [6 marks] Find the sum of all integers from 1 to 200 that leave remainder 3 when divided by 7.
Solution2929. The numbers are 3, 10, …, 199, which is (199 − 3) ÷ 7 + 1 = 29 terms. Sum = 29 × (3 + 199) ÷ 2 = 29 × 101 = 2929.
