Mensuration: Area, Volume and Surface Area

Syllabus: Area, volume and surface area of 2D and 3D shapes link to IB Mathematics: analysis and approaches SL 3.1 (distance, volume, surface area, angles between lines and planes) and SL 3.4 (arcs and sectors).

Guiding questions

  • How do we find perimeter and area of common 2D shapes?
  • How do we find volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres and pyramids?
  • How do arcs and sectors relate to the full circle?

Learning outcomes

  • Find perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, trapezia and circles.
  • Find arc length and sector area.
  • Find volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres and pyramids.
  • Use compound shapes and unit conversion.
  • Solve problems and check units.

2Study Notes

2D shapes. Rectangle A = lw. Triangle A = ½bh. Parallelogram A = bh. Trapezium A = ½(a + b)h. Circle: C = 2πr, A = πr².
Arc and sector. For angle θ°: arc = ⟦θ|360⟧ × 2πr, sector area = ⟦θ|360⟧ × πr².
Prism. V = area of cross-section × length. Cylinder: V = πr²h, curved surface 2πrh, total 2πrh + 2πr².
Cone: V = ⟦1|3⟧πr²h, curved surface πrl (l = slant height), l² = r² + h². Sphere: V = ⟦4|3⟧πr³, surface 4πr². Pyramid: V = ⟦1|3⟧ × base area × h.
Units. 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³. 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

Example 1 (circle). Find the area and circumference of a circle with radius 7 cm (use π = 3.14).
  1. Area = πr². 3.14 × 49 = 153.86
  2. Circumference = 2πr. 2 × 3.14 × 7 = 43.96
Answer: 153.86 cm² and 43.96 cm
Example 2 (trapezium). Parallel sides 6 and 10 cm, height 4 cm. Find the area.
  1. A = ½(a + b)h. ½ × 16 × 4 = 32
Answer: 32 cm²
Example 3 (sector). A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 75°. Find the arc length (exact).
  1. Arc = ⟦75|360⟧ × 2π × 12. ⟦5|24⟧ × 24π = 5π
Answer: 5π ≈ 15.7 cm
Example 4 (cylinder). A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume.
  1. V = πr²h. π × 25 × 12 = 300π
Answer: 300π ≈ 942 cm³
Example 5 (cone). A cone has radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Find the slant height and volume.
  1. l = √{6² + 8²}. 10
  2. V = ⟦1|3⟧πr²h. ⟦1|3⟧ × π × 36 × 8 = 96π
Answer: l = 10 cm, V = 96π ≈ 302 cm³
Example 6 (sphere). Find the volume and surface area of a sphere with radius 3 cm.
  1. V = ⟦4|3⟧πr³. ⟦4|3⟧ × π × 27 = 36π
  2. A = 4πr². 4π × 9 = 36π
Answer: V = 36π, A = 36π
Example 7 (units). A tank is 2 m by 1.5 m by 1 m. How many litres does it hold?
  1. Volume in m³. 2 × 1.5 × 1 = 3 m³
  2. Convert. 3 m³ = 3 × 1000 = 3000 litres
Answer: 3000 litres
Example 8 (compound). A solid is a cylinder (radius 4, height 10) with a hemisphere of radius 4 on top. Find its volume.
  1. Cylinder. π × 16 × 10 = 160π
  2. Hemisphere = ½ × ⟦4|3⟧πr³. ⟦2|3⟧ × π × 64 = ⟦128|3⟧π
  3. Add. 160π + ⟦128|3⟧π = ⟦608|3⟧π
Answer: ⟦608|3⟧π ≈ 637 cm³
Common mistakes.
① Using the slant height in volume (use the vertical height).
② Forgetting ⟦1|3⟧ for cones and pyramids.
③ Mixing cm and m.
④ Using diameter instead of radius.
⑤ Giving area in cm instead of cm².

3History & Facts

  • Archimedes (about 250 BCE): found the volume and surface area of the sphere and cylinder.
  • Ancient Egypt: the Moscow Papyrus gives the volume of a truncated pyramid.
  • Today: engineers, architects and packaging designers compute volume and area daily.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator unless stated. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] A cone has base radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find the exact volume. [3]
Mark schemeM1 V = ⟦1|3⟧πr²h; M1 ⟦1|3⟧ × π × 25 × 12; A1 100π cm³.
Question 2 [3 marks] A sector has radius 6 cm and angle 120°. Find the arc length and the sector area (exact). [3]
Mark schemeM1 ⟦1|3⟧ of the circle; A1 arc 4π cm; A1 area 12π cm².
Question 3 [3 marks] A cylinder has volume 450π cm³ and radius 5 cm. Find its height. [3]
Mark schemeM1 450π = π × 25 × h; M1 h = ⟦450|25⟧; A1 h = 18 cm.

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [8 marks] A water tank is a cylinder of radius 0.8 m and height 1.5 m. (a) Find its volume in m³ (3 s.f.). [2] (b) Find its capacity in litres. [1] (c) Water is poured in at 40 litres per minute. How long to fill it? [2] (d) Find the total outer surface area (closed). [3]
Mark scheme(a) M1 π × 0.8² × 1.5; A1 3.02 m³. (b) A1 3016 litres (≈ 3020). (c) M1 3016 ÷ 40; A1 75.4 minutes. (d) M1 2πrh = 7.54; M1 2πr² = 4.02; A1 11.6 m².
