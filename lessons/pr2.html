Addition of Probabilities

KISB Diploma Programme · Mathematics: analysis and approaches · Probability · addition rule · notes · lab · practice · exam-style questions · IA ideas

1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: IB Mathematics: analysis and approaches, SL 4.5 (combined events, mutually exclusive events, P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B), Venn diagrams).
Textbook references (sections only, no copied text)
Grade 10 book: Probability, section 3.2 “Addition of Probabilities”, and Revision Topic 12 “Statistics and Probability”. Related lessons on this site: “Union and Intersection of Sets” and “Probabilities of Simple Combined Events”.

Guiding questions

  • How do we find the probability that A or B happens?
  • Why must we subtract P(A ∩ B) when the events can overlap?
  • What changes if the events cannot happen together?

Learning outcomes

  • Use “or” to mean A ∪ B, and “and” to mean A ∩ B.
  • Decide whether two events are mutually exclusive.
  • Use P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) for mutually exclusive events.
  • Use P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) in general.
  • Use a Venn diagram with probabilities and answer “neither” questions.

2Study Notes

“A or B”. P(A ∪ B) is the probability that A happens, or B happens, or both. “A and B”. P(A ∩ B) is the probability that both happen.
Mutually exclusive events cannot happen at the same time, so P(A ∩ B) = 0. For example, “rolling a 2” and “rolling a 5” on one die. The Venn circles do not overlap.
Addition rule for mutually exclusive events: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).
General addition rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B). We subtract the overlap because it was counted twice. It is the same idea as n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B), divided by n(S).
Neither. P(neither A nor B) = 1 − P(A ∪ B). Exhaustive events cover the whole sample space together: P(A ∪ B) = 1.
Venn diagram with probabilities. Put P(A ∩ B) in the overlap first. Then P(A only) = P(A) − P(A ∩ B) and P(B only) = P(B) − P(A ∩ B). The outside region is 1 − P(A ∪ B). All four numbers add to 1.

Example 1 (mutually exclusive). A card is drawn from a 52-card pack. Find P(an ace or a king).
  1. Can a card be an ace and a king? No, so the events are mutually exclusive.
  2. P(ace) and P(king). ⟦4|52⟧ each.
  3. Add. ⟦4|52⟧ + ⟦4|52⟧ = ⟦8|52⟧ = ⟦2|13⟧.
Answer: ⟦2|13⟧
Example 2 (overlapping events). A card is drawn. Find P(a heart or a king).
  1. The king of hearts is in both events, so they overlap. Use the general rule.
  2. P(heart) = ⟦13|52⟧, P(king) = ⟦4|52⟧, P(heart and king) = ⟦1|52⟧. These come from counting cards.
  3. Substitute. ⟦13|52⟧ + ⟦4|52⟧ − ⟦1|52⟧ = ⟦16|52⟧.
  4. Simplify. ⟦4|13⟧.
Answer: ⟦4|13⟧
Example 3 (find the overlap). P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B).
  1. Write the rule. P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).
  2. Substitute. 0.7 = 0.5 + 0.4 − P(A ∩ B).
  3. Solve. P(A ∩ B) = 0.9 − 0.7 = 0.2.
Answer: 0.2
Example 4 (a Venn diagram of probabilities). Using Example 3, find P(A only), P(B only) and P(neither).
  1. P(A only) = P(A) − P(A ∩ B). 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.3.
  2. P(B only) = P(B) − P(A ∩ B). 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.2.
  3. P(neither) = 1 − P(A ∪ B). 1 − 0.7 = 0.3.
  4. Check: all regions add to 1. 0.3 + 0.2 + 0.2 + 0.3 = 1 ✓.
Answer: 0.3, 0.2 and 0.3.
Example 5 (dice). Two dice are rolled. Find P(sum = 7 or sum = 11).
  1. The sums cannot both occur, so the events are mutually exclusive. Add the probabilities.
  2. P(7) = ⟦6|36⟧ and P(11) = ⟦2|36⟧. Count from the sample space diagram.
  3. Add. ⟦8|36⟧ = ⟦2|9⟧.
Answer: ⟦2|9⟧
Example 6 (are they mutually exclusive?). P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.8. Are A and B mutually exclusive?
  1. If they were, P(A ∪ B) = 0.6 + 0.5. That is 1.1, which is impossible (greater than 1).
  2. Find the overlap. P(A ∩ B) = 0.6 + 0.5 − 0.8 = 0.3, which is not 0.
Answer: No, P(A ∩ B) = 0.3.
Example 7 (a survey). P(a student plays football) = 0.45 and P(plays tennis) = 0.30. Nobody plays both. Find P(football or tennis) and P(neither).
  1. Nobody plays both, so the events are mutually exclusive. P(F ∩ T) = 0.
  2. Add. 0.45 + 0.30 = 0.75.
  3. Neither. 1 − 0.75 = 0.25.
Answer: 0.75; 0.25.
Example 8 (red or face card). A card is drawn. Find P(a red card or a face card (J, Q, K)).
  1. Count each event. Red: 26 cards. Face: 12 cards. Red face cards: 6 cards.
  2. Use the rule. ⟦26|52⟧ + ⟦12|52⟧ − ⟦6|52⟧ = ⟦32|52⟧.
  3. Simplify. ⟦8|13⟧.
Answer: ⟦8|13⟧
Common mistakes.
① Adding P(A) + P(B) without subtracting the overlap when the events can happen together.
② Treating overlapping events as mutually exclusive.
③ Mixing up “or” (add) with “and” (multiply, next lesson).
④ Giving a probability greater than 1.
⑤ Forgetting “neither” = 1 − P(A ∪ B).

3History & Facts

  • Abraham de Moivre (1667–1754): published early rules for adding and multiplying probabilities in “The Doctrine of Chances”.
  • Andrey Kolmogorov (1903–1987): gave probability its modern axioms in 1933, including P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) for mutually exclusive events.
  • Today: insurers and doctors use the addition rule to combine risks.

5Real-World Applications

  • Insurance: the chance of damage by fire or flood.
  • Medicine: the chance that a patient has symptom A or symptom B.
  • Weather: the chance of rain or strong wind.
  • Games: the chance of drawing a heart or a king from a pack.
  • School: the chance a student studies French or Spanish.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] P(A) = 0.45, P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.15. Find (a) P(A ∪ B) [2] (b) P(neither A nor B) [1].
Mark scheme(a) M1 0.45 + 0.35 − 0.15; A1 0.65. (b) A1 0.35.
Question 2 [4 marks] A card is drawn from a pack of 52. Find P(a diamond or a face card). [4]
Mark schemeA1 P(diamond) = ⟦13|52⟧ and P(face) = ⟦12|52⟧; A1 overlap ⟦3|52⟧; M1 sum minus overlap; A1 ⟦22|52⟧ = ⟦11|26⟧.
Question 3 [3 marks] P(A) = 0.7 and P(B) = 0.6, and A and B are mutually exclusive. Explain why this is not possible. [3]
Mark schemeM1 P(A ∪ B) = 0.7 + 0.6; A1 1.3; R1 greater than 1, so impossible.

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [7 marks] In a school, P(a student is in the choir) = 0.3 and P(a student is in the band) = 0.25. P(a student is in both) = 0.1. (a) Draw a Venn diagram with probabilities. [3] (b) Find P(choir or band). [2] (c) A student is chosen. Find P(in the choir only). [1] (d) There are 600 students. Estimate the number in neither. [1]
Mark scheme(a) A1 overlap 0.1; A1 choir only 0.2 and band only 0.15; A1 outside 0.55. (b) M1 0.3 + 0.25 − 0.1; A1 0.45. (c) A1 0.2. (d) A1 600 × 0.55 = 330.
