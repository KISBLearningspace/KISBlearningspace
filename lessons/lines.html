Straight Lines, Distance and Midpoint

IB Diploma Programme · Mathematics: analysis and approaches · SL 2.1 · SL 3.1 · notes · lab · practice · exam-style questions · IA ideas

1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: Mathematics: analysis and approaches · SL 2.1 (Topic 2 Functions: different forms of the equation of a straight line, gradient, intercepts, parallel and perpendicular lines) and SL 3.1 (Topic 3 Geometry and trigonometry: the distance between two points and their midpoint, in two and three dimensions). HL students study the same content.
Book references

Oxford (Analysis and Approaches SL): Chapter 3 Modelling relationships: linear and quadratic functions, sections 3.1 Gradient of a linear function and 3.2 Linear functions; and Chapter 11 Relationships in space, section 11.1 The geometry of 3D shapes, for distance and midpoint in three dimensions.

Guiding questions

  • How does the gradient describe a line, and how do we find it from two points?
  • Why are there several forms of the equation of a line, and when is each most useful?
  • How does Pythagoras’ theorem give a formula for distance, and how does the midpoint formula follow from the idea of an average?

Learning outcomes

  • Find the gradient of a line from two points.
  • Write the equation of a line in the forms y = mx + c, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) and ax + by + d = 0, and convert between them.
  • Find x- and y-intercepts.
  • Use the gradient conditions for parallel (m₁ = m₂) and perpendicular (m₁ × m₂ = −1) lines.
  • Find the distance between two points and their midpoint in 2D and 3D.
  • Find the equation of a perpendicular bisector.

2Study Notes

Gradient from two points:
m = ⟦y₂ − y₁|x₂ − x₁⟧
FormEquationUse
Gradient-intercepty = mx + cread the gradient m and y-intercept (0, c)
Point-gradienty − y₁ = m(x − x₁)write the equation from a point and a gradient
Generalax + by + d = 0integer coefficients, answers in exam questions
Example 1. Find the equation of the line through A(1, 3) and B(5, 11), and its intercepts.
  1. Gradient. m = ⟦11 − 3|5 − 1⟧ = ⟦8|4⟧ = 2.
  2. Point-gradient form with A(1, 3). y − 3 = 2(x − 1).
  3. Expand and rearrange. y = 2x − 2 + 3 = 2x + 1. In general form, 2x − y + 1 = 0.
  4. y-intercept. Put x = 0: y = 1, so (0, 1).
  5. x-intercept. Put y = 0: 0 = 2x + 1, so x = −⟦1|2⟧.
Answer: y = 2x + 1; y-intercept (0, 1); x-intercept (−⟦1|2⟧, 0).
Example 2. Write 3x + 4y − 12 = 0 in the form y = mx + c and find the intercepts.
  1. Isolate the y term. 4y = −3x + 12.
  2. Divide by 4. y = −⟦3|4⟧x + 3.
  3. Read off. The gradient is −⟦3|4⟧ and the y-intercept is (0, 3).
  4. x-intercept. Put y = 0 in the original: 3x − 12 = 0, so x = 4.
Answer: y = −⟦3|4⟧x + 3; gradient −⟦3|4⟧; intercepts (0, 3) and (4, 0).
Parallel lines: m₁ = m₂.   Perpendicular lines: m₁ × m₂ = −1, so m₂ = −⟦1|m₁⟧.
Example 3. Find the line through (6, 1) perpendicular to y = 3x − 2.
  1. Gradient of the given line. m₁ = 3.
  2. Perpendicular gradient. m₁ × m₂ = −1, so m₂ = −⟦1|3⟧ (negative reciprocal).
  3. Point-gradient form with (6, 1). y − 1 = −⟦1|3⟧(x − 6).
  4. Simplify. y = −⟦1|3⟧x + 2 + 1 = −⟦1|3⟧x + 3.
  5. Check. At (6, 1): −⟦1|3⟧(6) + 3 = −2 + 3 = 1 ✓
Answer: y = −⟦1|3⟧x + 3
Distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂)
d = √{(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²}
In 3D add (z₂ − z₁)² under the root.
Midpoint
M = (⟦x₁ + x₂|2⟧, ⟦y₁ + y₂|2⟧)
In 3D add ⟦z₁ + z₂|2⟧.

The distance d is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.

Example 4 (2D). Find the distance and midpoint of A(1, 2) and B(7, 10).
  1. Distance formula. d = √{(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²}.
  2. Substitute. d = √{6² + 8²} = √{36 + 64} = √{100}.
  3. Simplify. d = 10.
  4. Midpoint = average of the coordinates. (⟦1 + 7|2⟧, ⟦2 + 10|2⟧) = (4, 6).
Answer: d = 10; midpoint (4, 6)
Example 5 (3D). Find the distance and midpoint of A(2, −1, 4) and B(6, 5, −2).
  1. Differences in each coordinate. 6 − 2 = 4, 5 − (−1) = 6, −2 − 4 = −6.
  2. Distance formula in 3D. d = √{4² + 6² + (−6)²} = √{16 + 36 + 36} = √{88}.
  3. Simplify. √{88} = √{4 × 22} = 2√{22} ≈ 9.38.
  4. Midpoint. (⟦2 + 6|2⟧, ⟦−1 + 5|2⟧, ⟦4 + (−2)|2⟧) = (4, 2, 1).
Answer: d = 2√{22} ≈ 9.38; midpoint (4, 2, 1)
Right angle test. Show that angle BAC is a right angle for A(0, 0), B(6, 2), C(−2, 6).
  1. Idea. Two lines are perpendicular if the product of their gradients is −1.
  2. Gradient of AB. mAB = ⟦2 − 0|6 − 0⟧ = ⟦1|3⟧.
  3. Gradient of AC. mAC = ⟦6 − 0|−2 − 0⟧ = −3.
  4. Multiply. ⟦1|3⟧ × (−3) = −1.
Answer: The product is −1, so AB ⊥ AC and the angle at A is 90°.
Perpendicular bisector. Find the perpendicular bisector of A(−1, 3) and B(5, 7).
  1. Midpoint of AB (the bisector passes through it). M = (⟦−1 + 5|2⟧, ⟦3 + 7|2⟧) = (2, 5).
  2. Gradient of AB. mAB = ⟦7 − 3|5 − (−1)⟧ = ⟦4|6⟧ = ⟦2|3⟧.
  3. Perpendicular gradient. −⟦3|2⟧ (negative reciprocal).
  4. Point-gradient form through M. y − 5 = −⟦3|2⟧(x − 2).
  5. Clear the fraction and rearrange. 2y − 10 = −3x + 6, so 3x + 2y − 16 = 0.
Answer: 3x + 2y − 16 = 0

3History & Facts

  • René Descartes (1637) and Pierre de Fermat: linked algebra and geometry, so a line can be written as an equation. The word “Cartesian” comes from Descartes’ Latin name, Cartesius.
  • Pythagoras’ theorem: the distance formula is this theorem in coordinates. The relationship was known in Babylon (clay tablet Plimpton 322, about 1800 BCE), in India (the Sulba Sutras) and in China long before Pythagoras.
  • Gradient in daily life: road signs give gradient as a percentage; a 10% slope rises 10 m for every 100 m travelled horizontally.
  • Fact: the midpoint is the average of the coordinates, which is why it is the balance point of a segment.

5Real-World Applications

  • Roads and ramps: a gradient of 1 in 12 is a common maximum for wheelchair ramps.
  • Maps and navigation: coordinates give distances and midpoints between places.
  • Drones and games: 3D coordinates are used to find distances between objects.
  • Construction: finding the centre of a beam is a midpoint calculation.
  • Data: a line of best fit is a straight-line model.

Paper 1 style (no calculator)

Question 1 [8 marks] The points A and B have coordinates (−1, 3) and (5, 7).
(a) Find the midpoint M of [AB]. [2]
(b) Find the gradient of AB. [1]
(c) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of [AB], giving your answer in the form ax + by + d = 0, where a, b, d ∈ ℤ. [3]
(d) Find the length of [AB], giving your answer in the form k√{13}. [2]
Mark scheme(a) M1 ⟦−1 + 5|2⟧, ⟦3 + 7|2⟧; A1 M(2, 5).
(b) A1 ⟦7 − 3|5 − (−1)⟧ = ⟦2|3⟧.
(c) A1 perpendicular gradient −⟦3|2⟧; M1 y − 5 = −⟦3|2⟧(x − 2); A1 3x + 2y − 16 = 0.
(d) M1 √{6² + 4²} = √{52}; A1 2√{13}.
Question 2 [5 marks] The line L₁ has equation 2x − 3y + 6 = 0.
(a) Find the gradient of L₁. [2]
(b) The line L₂ passes through (3, −1) and is perpendicular to L₁. Find the equation of L₂ in the form y = mx + c. [3]
Mark scheme(a) M1 y = ⟦2|3⟧x + 2; A1 gradient ⟦2|3⟧.
(b) A1 gradient −⟦3|2⟧; M1 y + 1 = −⟦3|2⟧(x − 3); A1 y = −⟦3|2⟧x + ⟦7|2⟧.
