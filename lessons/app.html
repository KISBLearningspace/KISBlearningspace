2Study Notes

Angle of elevation is measured up from the horizontal. Angle of depression is measured down from the horizontal. The angle of depression from A to B equals the angle of elevation from B to A (alternate angles).
Bearings. A bearing is measured clockwise from north, written with three digits (for example 040°). The bearing of A from B is the direction you face at B to look at A. Back bearing = bearing ± 180°.
Problem-solving plan. ① Sketch and label. ② Mark right angles and known values. ③ Split into triangles. ④ Pick the rule: right-angled → SOH CAH TOA; side–angle pair → sine rule; SAS or SSS → cosine rule. ⑤ Round only at the end and give units.
Example 1 (elevation). From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 32°. Find the height.
  1. Sketch. A right triangle: horizontal side 40 (adjacent), height h (opposite), angle 32° at the observer.
  2. Choose tan. tan 32° = ⟦h|40⟧.
  3. Solve. h = 40 tan 32° ≈ 25.0.
Answer: The tower is about 25.0 m high.
Example 2 (depression). From the top of a 60 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 25°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?
  1. Use alternate angles. The angle of depression is measured from the horizontal at the top. By alternate angles, the angle at the boat inside the triangle is also 25°.
  2. Choose tan at the boat. tan 25° = ⟦60|d⟧ (opposite 60, adjacent d).
  3. Solve. d = ⟦60|tan 25°⟧ ≈ 128.7.
Answer: About 128.7 m
Example 3 (bearings). A ship sails 12 km on a bearing of 050°, then 8 km on a bearing of 130°. Find the distance from the start and the bearing of the finish from the start.
  1. Find the interior angle at the turning point. The angle between the two legs is 180° − (130° − 50°) = 100°.
  2. Cosine rule for the distance d. d² = 12² + 8² − 2(12)(8) cos 100° = 144 + 64 + 33.3 = 241.3, so d ≈ 15.5 km.
  3. Use coordinates for the bearing. East: 12 sin 50° + 8 sin 130° ≈ 15.32. North: 12 cos 50° + 8 cos 130° ≈ 2.57.
  4. Bearing from the start. tan−1(⟦15.32|2.57⟧) ≈ 80.5°, so about 080° (angle from north).
  5. Bearing back to the start. 80.5° + 180° = 260.5°.
Answer: Distance ≈ 15.5 km; bearing ≈ 080°; the return bearing ≈ 260.5°.
Example 4 (two angles of elevation). From a point A the angle of elevation of a tower top is 25°. After walking 100 m towards the tower, to point B, it is 40°. Find the height.
  1. Let the height be h. The horizontal distance from A to the tower is ⟦h|tan 25°⟧ and from B is ⟦h|tan 40°⟧.
  2. The difference is the 100 m walked. ⟦h|tan 25°⟧ − ⟦h|tan 40°⟧ = 100.
  3. Factorise out h. h(⟦1|tan 25°⟧ − ⟦1|tan 40°⟧) = 100, that is h(2.1445 − 1.1918) = 100.
  4. Solve. h = ⟦100|0.9527⟧ ≈ 105.
  5. Check. The distances are 225.1 m and 125.1 m, which differ by 100 ✓
Answer: The tower is about 105 m high.
Example 5 (angle in a 3D shape). A vertical pole 6 m high stands at one corner of a rectangular field 8 m by 15 m. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the pole from the opposite corner.
  1. Find the horizontal distance (the field diagonal). √{8² + 15²} = √{289} = 17.
  2. Right triangle: pole = opposite, diagonal = adjacent. tan θ = ⟦6|17⟧.
  3. Find the angle. θ = tan−1(⟦6|17⟧) ≈ 19.4°.
Answer: θ ≈ 19.4°

Common mistakes

  • Calculator in radians instead of degrees.
  • Using the sine rule when the given angle is not opposite the given side.
  • Measuring a bearing from east or anticlockwise.
  • Rounding early.
Think: ① Why must a bearing have three digits? ② When does a problem need two triangles? ③ How could you measure the height of the school building without climbing it?

3History & Facts

  • Eratosthenes (about 240 BCE): measured the Earth’s circumference using shadow angles at two cities.
  • Polynesian navigators: crossed the Pacific using star bearings and swell directions long before compasses.
  • Gunter’s quadrant and sextants (1600s to 1700s): let sailors measure the angle of elevation of the Sun or a star to find latitude.
  • Fact: aircraft use bearings in a three-digit form so that “040” and “400” cannot be confused over the radio.

5Real-World Applications

  • Aviation and shipping: courses, bearings and wind drift.
  • Surveying and construction: heights of buildings, slopes of roads and angles of roofs.
  • Mountain rescue and drones: bearings and elevation to locate a position.
  • Astronomy: altitude angle of a star above the horizon.
  • Sport: launch angle of a ball and the angle of a camera to a stadium roof.

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. From a point 50 m from a building, the angle of elevation of its top is 38°. Find the height of the building.
    Answerh = 50 tan 38° ≈ 39.1 m.
  2. From the top of a 40 m tower, the angle of depression of a car is 18°. How far is the car from the foot of the tower?
    Answer⟦40|tan 18°⟧ ≈ 123 m.
  3. A hiker walks 5 km on a bearing of 060°, then 7 km on a bearing of 150°. How far is she from the start?
    AnswerInterior angle = 180° − 90° = 90°. Distance = √{25 + 49} = √{74} ≈ 8.60 km.
  4. Write down the bearing of Q from P if the bearing of P from Q is 225°.
    Answer225° − 180° = 045°.

Homework

  1. A ladder 6 m long leans against a wall and makes 65° with the ground. How high does it reach and how far is the foot from the wall?
    AnswerHeight = 6 sin 65° ≈ 5.44 m. Foot distance = 6 cos 65° ≈ 2.54 m.
  2. In HW 1 for safety the angle must be between 70° and 75°. What is the largest height the ladder can reach?
    Answer6 sin 75° ≈ 5.80 m.
  3. A plane flies 100 km on a bearing of 090° and then 150 km on a bearing of 200°. Find its distance from the start.
    AnswerAngle at the turning point = 180° − 110° = 70°. d² = 10000 + 22500 − 2(100)(150) cos 70° ≈ 22240; d ≈ 149 km.
  4. A hot-air balloon is seen at 20° elevation from A. From B, 200 m closer in line, the elevation is 35°. Find the height of the balloon.
    Answerh(⟦1|tan 20°⟧ − ⟦1|tan 35°⟧) = 200; h ≈ 200 ÷ 1.3186 ≈ 152 m.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style (no calculator)

Question 1 [6 marks] A vertical tower stands on horizontal ground. From a point A, 60 m from the foot, the angle of elevation of the top is 30°.
(a) Show that the height of the tower is 20√{3} m. [3]
(b) From a point B, in line with A and the tower, the angle of elevation is 60°. Find the distance AB. [3]
Mark scheme(a) M1 tan 30° = ⟦h|60⟧; A1 tan 30° = ⟦1|√{3}⟧; A1 h = ⟦60|√{3}⟧ = 20√{3}.
(b) M1 distance from B to the foot = ⟦20√{3}|tan 60°⟧ = 20; A1 AB = 60 − 20 (B between A and tower); A1 40 m.
Question 2 [5 marks] From the top of a 45 m cliff, two boats in line have angles of depression 30° and 45°. Find the exact distance between the boats.
Mark schemeM1 distances ⟦45|tan 30°⟧ and ⟦45|tan 45°⟧; A1 45√{3}; A1 45; M1 subtract; A1 45(√{3} − 1) ≈ 33.0 m.

Paper 2 style (GDC allowed)

Question 3 [9 marks] A boat leaves a harbour H and sails 12 km on a bearing of 050° to a buoy A. It then sails 8 km on a bearing of 130° to a point B.
(a) Show that angle HAB = 100°. [2]
(b) Find the distance HB. [3]
(c) Find the bearing of B from H. [3]
(d) Find the bearing of H from B. [1]
Mark scheme(a) M1 the bearing of H from A is 050° + 180° = 230°; R1 the angle between the directions 230° (to H) and 130° (to B) is 230° − 130° = 100°.
(b) M1 HB² = 12² + 8² − 2(12)(8) cos 100°; A1 241.3; A1 HB = 15.5 km.
(c) M1 sin ∠AHB = ⟦8 sin 100°|15.54⟧; A1 ∠AHB ≈ 30.5°; A1 bearing 050° + 30.5° = 080.5° (081° to the nearest degree).
(d) A1 080.5° + 180° = 260.5°.
