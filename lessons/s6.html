Regression: Line of Best Fit and Least Squares

Guiding questions

  • Which straight line is the “best” fit to a scatter diagram, and who decides?
  • What do the gradient and the y-intercept of a regression line mean in a real situation?
  • When is it safe to use the line to predict, and when is it not?

Learning outcomes

  • Draw a line of best fit by eye through the mean point.
  • Find the least squares regression line y = ax + b using the GDC and write it in context.
  • Interpret the gradient a and the intercept b.
  • Predict values, and distinguish interpolation from extrapolation.

2Study Notes

Line of best fit (by eye). A straight line drawn through the mean point (x̄, ȳ) with about as many points above it as below it. Different people draw slightly different lines, so we need a rule.
Residual. For a data point (xᵢ, yᵢ) and a line y = ax + b, the residual is the vertical distance:
residual = yᵢ − (axᵢ + b)
Points above the line have positive residuals and points below have negative residuals.
Least squares regression line of y on x. The line y = ax + b that makes the sum of the squares of the residuals as small as possible. With the GDC (two-variable statistics) we find a and b. The formulas are:
a = ⟦Sxy|Sxx⟧ = r · ⟦sy|sx⟧ ,   b = ȳ − a x̄
The line always passes through the mean point (x̄, ȳ).
Interpreting the line. The gradient a is the change in y for each increase of 1 in x. The y-intercept b is the predicted y when x = 0 (it may not make sense in context).
Interpolation and extrapolation. Interpolation: predicting y for an x inside the range of the data (usually reliable if |r| is large). Extrapolation: predicting outside the range of the data (unreliable, since the pattern may not continue).
Example 1 (find the line). Hours of study x and score y: (1, 52), (2, 55), (3, 61), (4, 60), (5, 68), (6, 72), (7, 75). Find the regression line of y on x.
  1. Enter the data in the GDC. List 1 = x, List 2 = y. Choose linear regression (y = ax + b).
  2. Read the values. a ≈ 3.93 and b ≈ 47.6.
  3. Write the equation with x and y. y = 3.93x + 47.6.
  4. Check with the mean point. (x̄, ȳ) = (4, 63.3). 3.93 × 4 + 47.6 = 63.3 ✓ (the line always passes through the mean point).
  5. (Optional) the formula behind the GDC. a = ⟦Sxy|Sxx⟧ = ⟦110|28⟧ ≈ 3.93 and b = ȳ − a x̄ = 63.29 − 3.93 × 4 ≈ 47.6.
Answer: y = 3.93x + 47.6 (r ≈ 0.983).
Example 2 (interpret). Interpret a and b in y = 3.93x + 47.6 (x = hours of study, y = score).
  1. The gradient a. It is the change in y for each increase of 1 in x: each extra hour of study is associated with about 3.9 more marks.
  2. The y-intercept b. It is the predicted y when x = 0: a student who studies 0 hours is predicted to score about 48 marks.
  3. Use the context words. Always say “predicted” or “associated with”.
Answer: a: about 3.9 more marks per hour; b: about 48 marks at 0 hours.
Example 3 (predict, interpolation). Predict the score for 5.5 hours of study.
  1. Substitute into the equation. y = 3.93 × 5.5 + 47.6.
  2. Calculate. 21.6 + 47.6 ≈ 69.2.
  3. Check the range. 5.5 is inside the data range 1 to 7.
Answer: About 69.2 marks. This is interpolation, so it is reasonably reliable.
Example 4 (extrapolation). Predict the score for 10 hours of study and comment.
  1. Substitute. y = 3.93 × 10 + 47.6 = 86.9.
  2. Check the range. 10 is outside the data range 1 to 7.
  3. Comment. This is extrapolation. The pattern may not continue (the score cannot exceed 100, and tiredness may reduce it).
Answer: About 86.9, but unreliable (extrapolation).
Example 5 (residual). Find the residual at x = 4, where the data value is y = 60.
  1. Predicted value. y = 3.93 × 4 + 47.6 ≈ 63.3.
  2. Residual = data − predicted. 60 − 63.3 = −3.3.
  3. Interpret the sign. Negative: the point is below the line.
  4. Least squares idea. The least squares line makes the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible (about 15.3 here).
Answer: Residual ≈ −3.3.
Example 6 (use y on x correctly). Can we use y = 3.93x + 47.6 to find the hours needed to score 80?
  1. What does the line minimise? The vertical distances (errors in y), so it is designed to predict y from x.
  2. What to avoid. Rearranging the equation to get x from y does not give the best prediction of x.
  3. What to do instead. Use the regression line of x on y (a different line, unless |r| = 1), and only if x is also a variable with scatter.
Answer: Use the regression line of x on y to predict x.
Think: ① Why do we square the residuals instead of just adding them? ② If the intercept predicts a negative height for a person at age 0, what does that tell us about the model? ③ Why is a prediction at x = 10 less trustworthy than at x = 5.5?

3History & Facts

  • Adrien-Marie Legendre (1805): published the method of least squares to fit the orbits of comets.
  • Carl Friedrich Gauss claimed he used it from 1795 and linked it to the normal distribution of errors.
  • Francis Galton (1880s): coined the word “regression” when studying how children’s heights return towards the average.
  • Today: least squares is the basis of machine learning, forecasting and many scientific models.

5Real-World Applications

  • Business: predicting sales from advertising spend.
  • Medicine: estimating a dose or growth from age and weight.
  • Climate science: trends in temperature over time (careful with extrapolation).
  • Sport: predicting race times from training.
  • Technology: machine-learning models begin with fitting a line.

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. A regression line is y = 2.5x + 10. Predict y when x = 8.
    Answery = 2.5 × 8 + 10 = 30.
  2. Interpret the gradient 2.5 if x is hours of practice and y is a test score.
    AnswerEach extra hour of practice is associated with about 2.5 more marks.
  3. The mean point of a data set is (3.5, 6) and the line is y = 1.2x + 1.8. Show that the line passes through the mean point.
    Answer1.2 × 3.5 + 1.8 = 4.2 + 1.8 = 6. ✓
  4. A point (4, 7) and the line y = 1.2x + 1.8. Find the residual.
    AnswerPredicted: 1.2 × 4 + 1.8 = 6.6. Residual = 7 − 6.6 = 0.4.

Homework

  1. Data lie between x = 10 and x = 28. Say whether predictions at x = 20 and x = 40 are interpolation or extrapolation.
    Answerx = 20: interpolation (inside the range). x = 40: extrapolation (outside the range).
  2. A line predicts height h = 0.6a + 80 (cm) for age a (years), data for ages 2 to 6. Interpret 0.6 and 80.
    AnswerGradient 0.6: about 0.6 cm of growth per year. Intercept 80: predicted height at age 0 (a newborn is about 50 cm, so this is not realistic).
  3. Why is the line of y on x not used to find x from y?
    AnswerIt minimises the vertical distances (errors in y). Predicting x requires the regression line of x on y, which is different.
  4. A least squares line has sum of squared residuals 15.3. Another line has 22.8. Which is the least squares line?
    AnswerThe one with 15.3. The least squares line has the smallest possible SSR.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [6 marks] A set of data has mean point (3.5, 6). The regression line of y on x is y = 1.2x + 1.8.
(a) Show that the line passes through the mean point. [2]
(b) Predict y when x = 5. [2]
(c) State what the gradient 1.2 means. [1]
(d) The data are for x from 1 to 6. State whether a prediction at x = 12 is reliable. [1]
Mark scheme(a) M1 substitute x = 3.5; A1 1.2 × 3.5 + 1.8 = 6, so the point is on the line.
(b) M1 1.2 × 5 + 1.8; A1 y = 7.8.
(c) A1 y increases by 1.2 for each 1 increase in x.
(d) R1 not reliable: extrapolation, outside the data range.
Question 2 [4 marks] The point (4, 7) is a data point. Using the line y = 1.2x + 1.8, find the residual and say whether the point is above or below the line. [4]
Mark schemeM1 predicted y = 1.2 × 4 + 1.8; A1 6.6; M1 7 − 6.6; A1 residual = 0.4 (positive, so the point is above the line).

Paper 2 style

Question 3 [9 marks] A shop records the temperature x (°C) and the number of ice creams sold y:
x1012151820222528
y3045486062758095
(a) Use the GDC to find the equation of the regression line of y on x. [3]
(b) Interpret the gradient. [1]
(c) Use the line to estimate the sales at 21°C. [2]
(d) Estimate the sales at 40°C and comment on its reliability. [3]
Mark scheme(a) M1 two-variable statistics on GDC; A1 a ≈ 3.32; A1 b ≈ −0.425, so y = 3.32x − 0.425.
(b) A1 sales increase by about 3.3 for each 1°C increase.
(c) M1 substitute x = 21; A1 ≈ 69 (69.35).
(d) M1 substitute x = 40; A1 ≈ 133 (132.5); R1 40°C is outside the data range (extrapolation), so unreliable (the shop may also sell out or few people go out in extreme heat).
