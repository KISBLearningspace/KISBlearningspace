Factors, Multiples, HCF and LCM

Guiding questions

  • What is the difference between a factor and a multiple?
  • How can prime numbers be used as the “building blocks” of every whole number?
  • Why do we need the HCF to simplify a fraction and the LCM to add fractions?

Learning outcomes

  • List the factors and multiples of a number.
  • Recognise prime numbers and write a number as a product of prime factors.
  • Find the HCF and LCM by listing, by prime factorisation and by a Venn diagram.
  • Use HCF × LCM = a × b for two numbers.
  • Solve word problems with HCF and LCM, and find the HCF and LCM of algebraic terms.

2Study Notes

Factor and multiple. A factor of 12 divides 12 exactly: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. A multiple of 12 is in its times table: 12, 24, 36, … Factors are smaller than (or equal to) the number; multiples are larger than (or equal to) it.
Prime number. A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, … The number 1 is not prime. 2 is the only even prime.
Prime factorisation. Every whole number greater than 1 is a product of primes, in only one way (apart from the order). Use a factor tree: split into two factors until all branches end in primes. Write repeated primes as powers: 180 = 2² × 3² × 5.
HCF (highest common factor). The largest number that divides both numbers exactly.
LCM (lowest common multiple). The smallest number that both numbers divide into exactly.
MethodHCFLCM
Listbiggest number in both factor listssmallest number in both multiple lists
Prime factorscommon primes, lowest powersall primes, highest powers
Venn diagrammultiply the overlapmultiply everything in the diagram
Useful facts. For two numbers a and b: HCF × LCM = a × b. If the HCF is 1 the numbers are coprime (they share no prime factor), and then the LCM is a × b.
Why it matters. To simplify a fraction we divide the top and bottom by their HCF. To add fractions we use the LCM of the denominators as the common denominator.
Example 1 (factors). List all the factors of 36.
  1. Test pairs that multiply to 36. 1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9, 6 × 6
  2. Write them in order. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
Answer: 9 factors
Example 2 (prime factorisation). Write 180 as a product of prime factors.
  1. Split with a factor tree. 180 = 18 × 10 = (2 × 3 × 3) × (2 × 5)
  2. Collect repeated primes as powers. 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5
Answer: 180 = 2² × 3² × 5
Example 3 (listing). Find the HCF and LCM of 12 and 18 by listing.
  1. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Largest common factor: 6
  2. Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36. Multiples of 18: 18, 36. First common multiple: 36
Answer: HCF = 6, LCM = 36
Example 4 (prime factors). Find the HCF and LCM of 84 and 90.
  1. Factorise. 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 90 = 2 × 3² × 5
  2. HCF: common primes, lowest powers. 2 × 3 = 6
  3. LCM: all primes, highest powers. 2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 1260
Answer: HCF = 6, LCM = 1260
Example 5 (check). Check the answer to Example 3 with HCF × LCM = a × b.
  1. HCF × LCM. 6 × 36 = 216
  2. a × b. 12 × 18 = 216 ✓
Answer: The product matches
Example 6 (LCM in context). Two buses leave the station at 8:00. One leaves every 12 minutes and the other every 18 minutes. When do they next leave together?
  1. Find the LCM of 12 and 18. 36
  2. Add 36 minutes to 8:00. 8:36
Answer: 8:36
Example 7 (HCF in context). A shop has 48 red and 72 blue ribbons. They are put into identical packs with no ribbons left over. What is the greatest number of packs, and what is in each pack?
  1. Greatest number of packs = HCF(48, 72). 48 = 2⁴ × 3 and 72 = 2³ × 3², so HCF = 2³ × 3 = 24
  2. Share out. 48 ÷ 24 = 2 red and 72 ÷ 24 = 3 blue
Answer: 24 packs, each with 2 red and 3 blue
Example 8 (three numbers). Find the LCM of 4, 6 and 10.
  1. Factorise. 4 = 2², 6 = 2 × 3, 10 = 2 × 5
  2. Highest power of each prime. 2² × 3 × 5
Answer: 60
Example 9 (algebra). Find the HCF and LCM of 12x²y and 18xy³.
  1. Numbers: HCF(12, 18) = 6 and LCM = 36. Letters: lowest power for HCF, highest for LCM
  2. HCF: x¹ and y¹. 6xy
  3. LCM: x² and y³. 36x²y³
Answer: HCF = 6xy, LCM = 36x²y³
Common mistakes.
① Calling 1 a prime number.
② Mixing up factor and multiple (HCF is never larger than the numbers, LCM is never smaller).
③ For the HCF, using the highest powers instead of the lowest.
④ Stopping a factor tree before every branch is prime.
⑤ Forgetting that the HCF × LCM rule works for two numbers only.

3History & Facts

  • Euclid (about 300 BCE): proved that there are infinitely many primes and described the Euclidean algorithm for the HCF.
  • Eratosthenes (about 240 BCE): the “sieve” for finding primes.
  • Today: huge primes protect online payments and messages (encryption).

5Real-World Applications

  • Timetables: when two buses, trains or lights next coincide (LCM).
  • Packing and sharing: the biggest equal groups with nothing left over (HCF).
  • Fractions: common denominators and simplest form.
  • Music: two rhythms of different lengths line up again after the LCM of their beats.
  • Security: encryption uses products of very large primes.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] Write 360 as a product of prime factors. [3]
Mark schemeM1 factor tree or repeated division; A1 correct primes 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 5; A1 2³ × 3² × 5.
Question 2 [4 marks] (a) Write 72 and 108 as products of prime factors. [2] (b) Hence find the HCF and LCM of 72 and 108. [2]
Mark scheme(a) A1 72 = 2³ × 3²; A1 108 = 2² × 3³. (b) A1 HCF = 2² × 3² = 36; A1 LCM = 2³ × 3³ = 216.
Question 3 [3 marks] Find the LCM of 4, 6 and 10. [3]
Mark schemeM1 prime factors 2², 2 × 3, 2 × 5; M1 highest powers; A1 60.

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [8 marks] (a) Write 180 and 240 as products of prime factors. [2] (b) Find the HCF of 180 and 240. [2] (c) Find the LCM of 180 and 240. [2] (d) Two lights flash every 180 seconds and every 240 seconds. They flash together at 12:00. Find the next time they flash together. [2]
Mark scheme(a) A1 180 = 2² × 3² × 5; A1 240 = 2⁴ × 3 × 5. (b) M1 lowest powers; A1 2² × 3 × 5 = 60. (c) M1 highest powers; A1 2⁴ × 3² × 5 = 720. (d) M1 720 s = 12 min; A1 12:12.
