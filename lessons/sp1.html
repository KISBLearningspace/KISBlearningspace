Cumulative Frequency Diagrams

1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: IB Mathematics: analysis and approaches, SL 4.2 (cumulative frequency graphs, finding median, quartiles and percentiles from the graph). This lesson starts from a frequency table and builds the graph step by step.
Textbook references (sections only, no copied text)
Grade 10 book: Data Analysis – Measures of Spread, section 2.1 “Cumulative Frequency Diagrams”, and Revision Topic 12 “Statistics and Probability”. Related lesson on this site: “Presenting Data: Histograms, Cumulative Frequency and Box Plots”.

Guiding questions

  • What does a cumulative frequency tell us that an ordinary frequency does not?
  • Why do we plot cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary?
  • How can one curve give us the median, the quartiles and any percentile?

Learning outcomes

  • Build a cumulative frequency table from a grouped frequency table.
  • Draw a cumulative frequency diagram (ogive) with the correct plotting points.
  • Read the median, quartiles, IQR and percentiles from the curve.
  • Estimate how many values are above or below a given value.
  • Check answers by counting and by the table.

2Study Notes

Grouped frequency table. Data are sorted into classes such as 0 < h ≤ 10, and the frequency f is how many values are in each class. The class upper boundary of 0 < h ≤ 10 is 10.
Cumulative frequency (CF). The running total of the frequencies up to and including a class. The last cumulative frequency equals the total number of values, n.
Cumulative frequency diagram (ogive). Plot each cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary (because the CF counts everything up to that value). Start at (lowest boundary, 0) and join the points with a smooth curve (or straight segments).
Reading the curve. Median: go to the CF value ⟦n|2⟧ on the vertical axis, across to the curve, then down to the horizontal axis. Lower quartile Q₁: use ⟦n|4⟧. Upper quartile Q₃: use ⟦3n|4⟧. k-th percentile: use ⟦k|100⟧ × n. IQR = Q₃ − Q₁.
Number of values above or below. Read the CF at the given value (this is how many are ≤ that value). The number above is n minus that.
StepWhat to do
1Add a cumulative frequency column (running total).
2Plot (upper boundary, CF), starting with (lowest boundary, 0).
3Join the points with a smooth curve.
4Read across from the CF, then down to the value.
Example 1 (cumulative frequency table). The heights of 40 plants (cm) are summarised. Complete the cumulative frequency column.
Class: 0<h≤10, 10<h≤20, 20<h≤30, 30<h≤40, 40<h≤50. Frequency: 4, 9, 14, 8, 5.
  1. First class: CF equals its own frequency. 4.
  2. Add the next frequency to the previous CF. 4 + 9 = 13.
  3. Continue adding. 13 + 14 = 27, then 27 + 8 = 35, then 35 + 5 = 40.
  4. Check the last CF equals the total. 40 = 4 + 9 + 14 + 8 + 5 ✓.
Answer: CF: 4, 13, 27, 35, 40.
Example 2 (plot the points). Write the plotting points for the plant data in Example 1.
  1. Use the upper boundary of each class with its CF. (10, 4), (20, 13), (30, 27), (40, 35), (50, 40).
  2. Add the starting point: no plants are shorter than the lowest boundary 0. (0, 0).
  3. Join the points with a smooth curve. The curve rises slowly, steeply in the middle, then slowly again.
Answer: Points (0, 0), (10, 4), (20, 13), (30, 27), (40, 35), (50, 40).
Example 3 (median). Use the plant data to estimate the median height.
  1. n = 40, so the median is at the CF value ⟦40|2⟧. 20.
  2. On the curve, find where CF = 20. It is between (20, 13) and (30, 27). Reading the graph gives about 25 cm.
  3. Check by proportion in the class. 20 + ⟦20 − 13|27 − 13⟧ × 10 = 20 + 5 = 25.
Answer: Median ≈ 25 cm.
Example 4 (quartiles and IQR). Estimate Q₁, Q₃ and the IQR for the plant data.
  1. Q₁ is at CF = ⟦40|4⟧ = 10. This is between (10, 4) and (20, 13): 10 + ⟦10 − 4|13 − 4⟧ × 10 ≈ 16.7 cm.
  2. Q₃ is at CF = ⟦3×40|4⟧ = 30. This is between (30, 27) and (40, 35): 30 + ⟦30 − 27|35 − 27⟧ × 10 = 33.75 cm.
  3. IQR = Q₃ − Q₁. 33.75 − 16.7 ≈ 17.1 cm.
Answer: Q₁ ≈ 16.7, Q₃ ≈ 33.8, IQR ≈ 17 cm.
Example 5 (a percentile). Estimate the 90th percentile of the plant heights.
  1. Find 90% of n. 0.9 × 40 = 36.
  2. Find the height where CF = 36. It is between (40, 35) and (50, 40). 40 + ⟦36 − 35|40 − 35⟧ × 10 = 42.
  3. Interpret. About 90% of the plants are 42 cm or shorter.
Answer: 90th percentile ≈ 42 cm.
Example 6 (how many above a value?). Estimate how many plants are taller than 35 cm.
  1. Find the CF at 35 cm (between (30, 27) and (40, 35)). 27 + ⟦35 − 30|40 − 30⟧ × 8 = 27 + 4 = 31.
  2. So 31 plants are 35 cm or shorter. CF(35) = 31.
  3. Taller than 35 cm = total − 31. 40 − 31 = 9.
Answer: About 9 plants.
Example 7 (find frequencies from a CF table). A CF table has values 6, 20, 41, 50 at upper boundaries 10, 20, 30, 40. Find the frequency of each class.
  1. The first frequency is the first CF. 6.
  2. Subtract successive CF values. 20 − 6 = 14, then 41 − 20 = 21, then 50 − 41 = 9.
  3. Check the total. 6 + 14 + 21 + 9 = 50 ✓.
Answer: Frequencies 6, 14, 21, 9.
Common mistakes.
① Plotting at the class midpoint. Use the upper boundary.
② Forgetting the starting point (lowest boundary, 0).
③ Using n instead of ⟦n|2⟧ for the median.
④ Reading Q₁ at ⟦n|2⟧ by mistake: Q₁ uses ⟦n|4⟧.
⑤ Not stating that readings from the graph are estimates.

3History & Facts

  • Florence Nightingale (1820–1910): used statistical graphs to show that most soldiers in the Crimean War died from disease, which changed hospital practice.
  • Francis Galton (1822–1911): developed the idea of percentiles and quartiles to describe human measurements.
  • The word “ogive” comes from architecture: the S-shaped curve looks like a pointed arch.
  • Today: growth charts for children use percentile curves based on cumulative data.

5Real-World Applications

  • Growth charts: a child at the 75th percentile for height is taller than 75% of children of the same age.
  • Exam results: “the median mark” and “top 10%” come from cumulative frequency.
  • Income and house prices: reports often give the median and percentiles.
  • Quality control: how many items are lighter than a limit.
  • Sport: race times, where the 20th percentile is a fast time.

6IB Links: TOK, International-mindedness & ATL

  • TOK: A reading from the curve is an estimate. How do we decide when an estimate is “good enough”?
  • International-mindedness: percentile growth charts are used by health services worldwide, often adapted to local populations.
  • ATL: communication (labelling axes and units), thinking (checking with the table).
  • IA ideas: collect data (reaction times, heights, sleep hours) and use cumulative frequency to compare groups.

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. Frequencies 3, 7, 10, 5 (n = 25). Find the cumulative frequencies.
    Answer3, 10, 20, 25.
  2. For n = 60, at which CF value do you read the median, Q₁ and Q₃?
    Answer30, 15 and 45.
  3. A CF table has 5, 18, 40 at upper boundaries 10, 20, 30. Find the frequencies.
    Answer5, 13, 22.
  4. Why do we plot CF against the upper boundary?
    AnswerThe CF counts every value up to that boundary.

Homework

  1. Class 0–20: 6, 20–40: 14, 40–60: 22, 60–80: 8 (n = 50). Write the cumulative frequencies.
    Answer6, 20, 42, 50.
  2. Using the data above, estimate the median.
    AnswerCF = 25, between (40, 20) and (60, 42): 40 + ⟦5|22⟧ × 20 ≈ 44.5.
  3. For n = 80, how many values are above the 75th percentile?
    Answer25% of 80 = 20 values.
  4. The 10th percentile of 40 values is at which CF?
    Answer0.10 × 40 = 4.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] The table shows the time (t minutes) for 50 students to finish a task. 0<t≤10: 5; 10<t≤20: 13; 20<t≤30: 22; 30<t≤40: 10. Complete the cumulative frequency column. [3]
Mark schemeM1 running totals; A1 5, 18; A1 40, 50.
Question 2 [4 marks] Using the data in Question 1, estimate the median time. [4]
Mark schemeM1 n/2 = 25; M1 locate between (20, 18) and (30, 40); M1 20 + ⟦7|22⟧ × 10; A1 ≈ 23.2 minutes.
Question 3 [3 marks] A CF graph shows 80 values. The curve reaches CF = 20 at 14, CF = 40 at 22 and CF = 60 at 31. Find the median and the IQR (using these readings only if possible). [3]
Mark schemeA1 median = 22 (CF 40); A1 Q₁ = 14 (CF 20); A1 Q₃ = 31 (CF 60); so IQR = 31 − 14 = 17 (award A1 for the IQR).

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [8 marks] The masses (kg) of 60 parcels: 0<m≤5: 6; 5<m≤10: 14; 10<m≤15: 22; 15<m≤20: 12; 20<m≤25: 6. (a) Write the cumulative frequency table. [2] (b) Draw the cumulative frequency curve. [3] (c) Estimate the median. [1] (d) Estimate how many parcels weigh more than 18 kg. [2]
Mark scheme(a) A1 6, 20, 42; A1 54, 60. (b) A1 correct points at upper boundaries; A1 (0, 0) included; A1 smooth curve. (c) A1 CF = 30: 10 + ⟦10|22⟧ × 5 ≈ 12.3 kg. (d) M1 CF(18) = 42 + ⟦3|5⟧ × 12 = 49.2; A1 60 − 49.2 ≈ 11 parcels.
