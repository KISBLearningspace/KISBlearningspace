Transformations of Functions

KISB Diploma Programme · Mathematics: analysis and approaches · SL 2.11 · notes · lab · practice · exam-style questions · IA ideas

1IB Syllabus Link & Learning Outcomes

Syllabus: Mathematics: analysis and approaches · SL 2.11 (Topic 2 Functions: transformations of graphs: translations y = f(x) + b and y = f(x − a); reflections in both axes y = −f(x) and y = f(−x); vertical stretch y = p f(x); horizontal stretch y = f(qx); and composite transformations). Guiding questions

  • How can one graph be used to draw a whole family of graphs?
  • Why does y = f(x − 3) move the graph to the right, when the “−” seems to suggest left?
  • Does the order in which we apply transformations matter?

Learning outcomes

  • Describe and apply translations, reflections and stretches of a graph.
  • Match each transformation with its effect on the coordinates of a point.
  • Write the equation of a transformed graph, for example y = −2f(x + 1) + 3.
  • Find the image of a given point (for example a vertex or an intercept) after one or more transformations.
  • Apply composite transformations in the correct order.

2Study Notes

Idea: start with the graph of y = f(x) (the “parent” graph). Changing the equation in a particular way moves, flips or stretches every point of the graph in the same way.
EquationTransformationA point (x, y) moves to
y = f(x) + btranslation b units up (down if b < 0)(x, y + b)
y = f(x − a)translation a units right (left if a < 0)(x + a, y)
y = −f(x)reflection in the x-axis(x, −y)
y = f(−x)reflection in the y-axis(−x, y)
y = p f(x)vertical stretch, scale factor p(x, py)
y = f(qx)horizontal stretch, scale factor ⟦1|q⟧(⟦x|q⟧, y)
Why does f(x − a) move right? The new graph needs the same output as the old one, so its input must be larger by a. The old point (x, y) becomes (x + a, y). Changes inside the brackets act on x and do the “opposite” of what you expect; changes outside act on y and do what you expect.
Example 1 (translation). Describe the graph of y = (x − 2)² + 1 compared with y = x².
  1. Start from y = x². Its vertex is (0, 0).
  2. x − 2 inside the bracket. Moves the graph 2 units right (the sign is opposite to what you might expect).
  3. +1 outside. Moves the graph 1 unit up.
Answer: The graph is translated 2 right and 1 up, so the new vertex is (2, 1).
Example 2 (points). The point (2, 5) lies on y = f(x). Find the image points on (a) y = 2f(x) + 1, (b) y = f(x + 3) − 4, (c) y = f(⟦1|2⟧x), (d) y = −f(−x).
  1. Rules. Changes outside f affect y; changes inside affect x, in the opposite way.
  2. (a) y: 2(5) + 1 = 11; x stays 2. Image (2, 11).
  3. (b) x: 2 − 3 = −1 (x + 3 moves left 3); y: 5 − 4 = 1. Image (−1, 1).
  4. (c) ⟦1|2⟧x inside doubles the x-value (horizontal stretch, scale factor 2): x = 4; y stays 5. Image (4, 5).
  5. (d) −f(−x) reflects in both axes: x = −2 and y = −5. Image (−2, −5).
Answer: (a) (2, 11) (b) (−1, 1) (c) (4, 5) (d) (−2, −5)
Example 3 (composite). The point (4, −2) lies on y = f(x). Find its image on y = 3f(x − 2) + 1.
  1. Deal with x (inside). x − 2 moves right 2: 4 + 2 = 6.
  2. Deal with y (outside). Multiply by 3 then add 1: 3(−2) + 1 = −6 + 1 = −5.
Answer: (6, −5)
Order of transformations. For y = p f(q(x − a)) + b, the point (x, y) goes to
( ⟦x|q⟧ + a , py + b )
Stretch and reflect first, translate last. Work on the y-values in the usual order of operations (multiply, then add). For the x-values, undo what is done to x (divide, then add).
Example 4 (order matters). Start with y = x². Compare (i) reflect in the x-axis, then translate 2 up, with (ii) translate 2 up, then reflect in the x-axis.
  1. (i) Reflect. y = −x².
  2. (i) Translate 2 up. Add 2: y = −x² + 2.
  3. (ii) Translate 2 up. y = x² + 2.
  4. (ii) Reflect (multiply the whole function by −1). y = −(x² + 2) = −x² − 2.
Answer: The graphs differ: (i) has maximum (0, 2) and (ii) has maximum (0, −2).
Example 5 (describe). Describe how y = f(x) becomes y = −3f(x + 2) − 1.
  1. Inside the bracket. x + 2: translate 2 units left.
  2. Outside, in order. Multiply by 3: vertical stretch, scale factor 3. The minus sign: reflect in the x-axis. Then −1: translate 1 unit down.
Answer: Translate 2 left; vertical stretch by 3; reflect in the x-axis; translate 1 down.
Example 6 (hidden stretch). Describe y = f(2x + 6).
  1. Factorise inside the bracket. 2x + 6 = 2(x + 3), so y = f(2(x + 3)).
  2. Horizontal stretch. Multiplying x by 2 gives a horizontal stretch with scale factor ⟦1|2⟧.
  3. Translation. The (x + 3) then moves the graph 3 units left.
  4. Check with a point. The point (4, y) on f needs 2x + 6 = 4, so x = −1. The rule gives ⟦4|2⟧ − 3 = −1 ✓
Answer: Horizontal stretch with scale factor ⟦1|2⟧, then a translation 3 left.
Even and odd functions. If f(−x) = f(x) the graph is unchanged by reflection in the y-axis (even, like x²). If f(−x) = −f(x) the graph looks the same after a half-turn about the origin (odd, like x³).

3History & Facts

  • René Descartes (1637): coordinates let a graph be drawn from an equation, so shapes could be moved and compared algebraically.
  • Felix Klein (1872, Erlangen Programme): proposed that geometry is the study of properties that stay the same under transformations such as translations and reflections.
  • Islamic and Thai art: repeating geometric patterns in mosques, palaces and temple decoration are built from translations, reflections and rotations of one motif.
  • Fact: computer graphics store every image as numbers and move it with exactly these rules. Flipping a sprite is f(−x), zooming in is a stretch.

5Real-World Applications

  • Computer graphics and games: every sprite is moved, flipped and scaled with translations, reflections and stretches.
  • Sound: a louder note is a vertical stretch of the wave; a higher pitch is a horizontal stretch; a delay is a translation.
  • Economics: a shift of a demand curve (for example because of a rise in income) is a translation.
  • Architecture and engineering: arches and cables are parabolas whose size and position are set by transformations.
  • Science: the same graph shape with different units or scales can describe many experiments.

6IB Links: TOK, International-mindedness & ATL

  • TOK: Klein defined geometry by what stays the same under transformations. Do we understand things better by studying what changes, or what stays invariant?

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. Describe the transformation that maps y = f(x) to y = f(x) − 5.
    AnswerA translation 5 units down.
  2. The point (3, −2) is on y = f(x). Find its image on (a) y = f(x − 4), (b) y = −f(x).
    Answer(a) (7, −2). (b) (3, 2).
  3. Describe the transformations in y = f(−x) + 2.
    AnswerReflection in the y-axis, then translation 2 units up.
  4. Write the equation of y = x² after a translation 2 units left and 3 units down.
    Answery = (x + 2)² − 3.

Homework

  1. The point (6, 4) lies on y = f(x). Find its image on (a) y = 2f(x), (b) y = f(3x), (c) y = f(x + 1) − 3.
    Answer(a) (6, 8). (b) (2, 4). (c) (5, 1).
  2. Describe the transformations that map y = f(x) to y = −⟦1|2⟧f(x − 3) + 4.
    AnswerTranslate 3 right; vertical stretch, scale factor ⟦1|2⟧; reflect in the x-axis; translate 4 up.
  3. The vertex of y = f(x) is (−1, 5). Find the vertex of y = f(2x) − 3.
    Answerx-coordinate: −⟦1|2⟧; y-coordinate: 5 − 3 = 2. Vertex (−⟦1|2⟧, 2).
  4. The graph of y = √{x} is reflected in the y-axis and then translated 4 units right. Find the equation.
    AnswerReflection: y = √{−x}. Replace x by (x − 4): y = √{−(x − 4)} = √{4 − x}.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style (no calculator)

Question 1 [6 marks] The points A(−2, 1), B(0, 4) and C(3, −2) lie on the graph of y = f(x). The graph of y = g(x) is obtained from y = f(x) by a vertical stretch with scale factor 2, followed by a translation of 1 unit left and 3 units up.
(a) Write down g(x) in terms of f. [2]
(b) Find the coordinates of the images of A, B and C. [3]
(c) A different function is h(x) = 2(f(x) + 3). Write down the image of A on y = h(x). [1]
Mark scheme(a) M1 for 2f(x) or f(x + 1); A1 g(x) = 2f(x + 1) + 3.
(b) A1 A′(−3, 5); A1 B′(−1, 11); A1 C′(2, −1).
(c) A1 (−2, 8), since 2(1 + 3) = 8 and x does not change.
Question 2 [6 marks] Let f(x) = x². The graph of g is obtained from the graph of f by a reflection in the x-axis, then a translation of 4 units right, then a translation of 1 unit up.
(a) Find g(x) in the form −(x − h)² + k. [3]
(b) Write down the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of g. [2]
(c) Write down the range of g. [1]
Mark scheme(a) M1 reflection: −x²; M1 replace x by x − 4; A1 g(x) = −(x − 4)² + 1.
(b) A1 A1 (4, 1).
(c) A1 g(x) ≤ 1.

Paper 2 style (GDC allowed)

Question 3 [7 marks] A suspension bridge cable hangs between two towers. With the origin at the lowest point of a flat road model, the cable is modelled by h(x) = 0.01x² + 5, where x and h are in metres and −50 ≤ x ≤ 50.
(a) Describe the transformation that maps the graph of f(x) = 0.01x² to the graph of h. [2]
(b) Find the height of the cable above the road at the towers. [2]
(c) A wider bridge uses k(x) = h(⟦1|2⟧x), a horizontal stretch of h with scale factor 2. Find k(x) and the height of its cable at x = 50. [3]
Mark scheme(a) M1 translation; A1 5 units up (vertical translation by 5).
(b) M1 h(50) = 0.01(2500) + 5; A1 30 m.
(c) A1 k(x) = 0.01(⟦x|2⟧)² + 5 = 0.0025x² + 5; M1 substitute x = 50; A1 0.0025(2500) + 5 = 11.25 m.
