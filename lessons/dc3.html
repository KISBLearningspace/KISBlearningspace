Chain, Product and Quotient Rules

Guiding questions

  • How do we differentiate a function inside another function?
  • How do we differentiate a product or a quotient of two functions?
  • How can we check an answer by testing a value?

Learning outcomes

  • Use the chain rule ⟦dy|dx⟧ = ⟦dy|du⟧ × ⟦du|dx⟧.
  • Differentiate sin(ax + b), e^(ax + b), ln(ax + b) and (ax + b)ⁿ.
  • Use the product rule (uv)′ = u′v + uv′.
  • Use the quotient rule ⟦u′v − uv′|v²⟧.
  • Choose the right rule for a given function.

2Study Notes

Chain rule
y = f(g(x)) ⇒ ⟦dy|dx⟧ = f′(g(x)) × g′(x)
Differentiate the outside (keeping the inside), then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
Common cases. ⟦d|dx⟧ sin(ax + b) = a cos(ax + b). ⟦d|dx⟧ e^(ax + b) = a e^(ax + b). ⟦d|dx⟧ ln(ax + b) = ⟦a|ax + b⟧. ⟦d|dx⟧ (ax + b)ⁿ = n a (ax + b)ⁿ⁻¹.
Product rule
y = uv ⇒ ⟦dy|dx⟧ = u′v + uv′
“First times derivative of second, plus second times derivative of first.”
Quotient rule
y = ⟦u|v⟧ ⇒ ⟦dy|dx⟧ = ⟦u′v − uv′|v²⟧
Order matters in the numerator: u′v first, then subtract uv′.
SituationRule
function of a functionchain
two functions multipliedproduct
one function divided by anotherquotient
Example 1 (chain, power). Differentiate y = (3x + 1)⁵.
  1. Outer: u⁵, inner: u = 3x + 1. ⟦dy|du⟧ = 5u⁴, ⟦du|dx⟧ = 3
  2. Multiply. 15(3x + 1)⁴
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = 15(3x + 1)⁴
Example 2 (chain, standard functions). Differentiate (a) sin(2x), (b) e^(3x), (c) ln(5x).
  1. (a) 2cos(2x)
  2. (b) 3e^(3x)
  3. (c) ⟦5|5x⟧ ⟦1|x⟧
Answer: (a) 2cos 2x (b) 3e³ˣ (c) ⟦1|x⟧
Example 3 (chain with x²). Differentiate y = (x² + 1)³.
  1. Outer 3u², inner derivative 2x. 3(x² + 1)² × 2x
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = 6x(x² + 1)²
Example 4 (root). Differentiate y = √{4x − 3}.
  1. Write as (4x − 3)^(½). ½(4x − 3)^(−½) × 4
  2. Simplify. ⟦2|√{4x − 3}⟧
Answer: ⟦2|√{4x − 3}⟧
Example 5 (product). Differentiate y = x²eˣ.
  1. u = x², v = eˣ. u′ = 2x, v′ = eˣ
  2. u′v + uv′. 2xeˣ + x²eˣ
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = xeˣ(2 + x)
Example 6 (product, trig). Differentiate y = x sin x.
  1. u = x, v = sin x. u′ = 1, v′ = cos x
  2. Apply. sin x + x cos x
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = sin x + x cos x
Example 7 (quotient). Differentiate y = ⟦x|x + 1⟧.
  1. u = x, v = x + 1. u′ = 1, v′ = 1
  2. ⟦u′v − uv′|v²⟧. ⟦(x + 1) − x|(x + 1)²⟧
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = ⟦1|(x + 1)²⟧
Example 8 (product with ln). Differentiate y = x ln x and find the gradient at x = e.
  1. u = x, v = ln x. u′ = 1, v′ = ⟦1|x⟧
  2. u′v + uv′. ln x + 1
  3. At x = e. 1 + 1 = 2
Answer: ⟦dy|dx⟧ = ln x + 1; gradient 2
Common mistakes.
① Forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inside function.
② Differentiating a product as (u′)(v′).
③ Mixing up the order in the quotient rule numerator.
④ Forgetting to square the denominator in the quotient rule.
⑤ Using the chain rule when the product rule is needed (e.g. x sin x).

3History & Facts

  • Gottfried Leibniz (1676): stated the product rule in his notebooks.
  • Chain rule: follows naturally from Leibniz’s notation ⟦dy|dx⟧ = ⟦dy|du⟧ × ⟦du|dx⟧.
  • Today: these rules are the core of backpropagation, the method that trains neural networks.

5Real-World Applications

  • Physics: rates of change that depend on other changing quantities.
  • Economics: revenue = price × quantity changes both ways.
  • Biology: drug concentration models.
  • Machine learning: neural networks use the chain rule repeatedly.
  • Engineering: oscillations with decay: e^(−t) sin t.

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] Differentiate y = (5x² − 3)⁴. [3]
Mark schemeM1 chain rule; A1 4(5x² − 3)³ × 10x; A1 40x(5x² − 3)³.
Question 2 [4 marks] Differentiate y = x³ sin 2x. [4]
Mark schemeM1 product rule; A1 u′ = 3x²; A1 v′ = 2cos 2x; A1 3x² sin 2x + 2x³ cos 2x.
Question 3 [4 marks] Given f(x) = ⟦2x + 1|x − 3⟧, find f′(x) and the gradient at x = 4. [4]
Mark schemeM1 quotient rule; A1 ⟦2(x − 3) − (2x + 1)|(x − 3)²⟧; A1 f′(x) = −⟦7|(x − 3)²⟧; A1 f′(4) = −7.

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [8 marks] The concentration of a drug in the blood is C(t) = 20t e^(−0.5t) mg/L, t in hours. (a) Find C′(t). [3] (b) Find the time when C′(t) = 0. [2] (c) Find the maximum concentration. [2] (d) State the rate of change at t = 4. [1]
Mark scheme(a) M1 product rule; A1 20e^(−0.5t) + 20t(−0.5)e^(−0.5t); A1 20e^(−0.5t)(1 − 0.5t). (b) M1 1 − 0.5t = 0; A1 t = 2 hours. (c) M1 C(2) = 40e^(−1); A1 14.7 mg/L. (d) A1 C′(4) = 20e^(−2)(−1) = −2.71 mg/L per hour.
