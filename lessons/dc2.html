2Study Notes

Power rule
If f(x) = axⁿ then f′(x) = a n xⁿ⁻¹
Bring the power down to multiply, then reduce the power by 1. A constant has derivative 0, and ⟦d|dx⟧(ax) = a.
Sums and multiples. Differentiate term by term: (f ± g)′ = f′ ± g′ and (kf)′ = kf′.
Rewrite first. √x = x^(½), ⟦1|x⟧ = x⁻¹, ⟦3|x²⟧ = 3x⁻². Expand brackets and split fractions before differentiating.
Standard derivatives
sin x → cos x cos x → −sin x eˣ → eˣ ln x → ⟦1|x⟧
They hold when x is in radians.

Example 1 (power rule). Differentiate (a) x⁵, (b) 3x⁴, (c) 7x, (d) 9.
  1. (a) bring 5 down. 5x⁴
  2. (b) 3 × 4. 12x³
  3. (c) 7
  4. (d) constant. 0
Answer: (a) 5x⁴ (b) 12x³ (c) 7 (d) 0
Example 2 (polynomial). Differentiate f(x) = 2x³ − 5x² + 4x − 7.
  1. Term by term. 6x² − 10x + 4 + 0
Answer: f′(x) = 6x² − 10x + 4
Example 3 (root). Differentiate f(x) = √x.
  1. Write as a power. x^(½)
  2. Power rule. ½ x^(−½)
  3. Rewrite. ⟦1|2√{x}⟧
Answer: f′(x) = ⟦1|2√{x}⟧
Example 4 (fraction). Differentiate f(x) = ⟦3|x²⟧.
  1. Write as 3x⁻². Power rule: 3 × (−2)x⁻³
  2. Rewrite. −6x⁻³ = −⟦6|x³⟧
Answer: f′(x) = −⟦6|x³⟧
Example 5 (expand first). Differentiate (a) (x + 2)², (b) x(x − 3), (c) ⟦x² + 3x|x⟧.
  1. (a) x² + 4x + 4. 2x + 4
  2. (b) x² − 3x. 2x − 3
  3. (c) divide by x: x + 3. 1
Answer: (a) 2x + 4 (b) 2x − 3 (c) 1
Example 6 (trig, exponential, log). Differentiate (a) 3 sin x − 2eˣ, (b) 5 ln x + 2 cos x.
  1. (a) 3 cos x − 2eˣ
  2. (b) ⟦5|x⟧ − 2 sin x
Answer: (a) 3cos x − 2eˣ (b) ⟦5|x⟧ − 2sin x
Example 7 (gradient at a point). f(x) = x³ − 6x. Find the gradient at x = 2.
  1. f′(x) = 3x² − 6. f′(2) = 12 − 6
Answer: 6
Example 8 (zero gradient). Find where f(x) = x² − 6x + 5 has gradient 0.
  1. f′(x) = 2x − 6. 2x − 6 = 0
  2. Solve. x = 3
Answer: x = 3 (the point is (3, −4))
Common mistakes.
① Forgetting to reduce the power by 1.
② Differentiating ⟦1|x²⟧ as ⟦1|2x⟧ (rewrite as x⁻² first).
③ Differentiating a constant as the constant itself (it is 0).
④ d/dx(cos x) = sin x (the sign is negative).
⑤ Using degrees: the sin and cos rules need radians.

3History & Facts

  • Isaac Newton (1666): found the power rule while studying motion and gravity.
  • Leonhard Euler (1700s): showed that eˣ is its own derivative.
  • Today: symbolic software applies these rules instantly, but understanding them helps check results.

5Real-World Applications

  • Physics: velocity from displacement.
  • Engineering: slope of a ramp or a curve in a road.
  • Economics: marginal cost and revenue.
  • Population models: eˣ models growth.
  • Signal processing: sin and cos for waves.

6IB Links: TOK, International-mindedness & ATL

  • TOK: Rules such as the power rule save time but hide the idea of a limit. When do we trust a rule over its proof?
  • International-mindedness: calculus rules were refined by mathematicians in many countries.
  • ATL: thinking (rewrite before you differentiate), communication (clear notation).

10Exam-style Questions

Marks: M = method, A = accuracy, R = reasoning. No calculator. Open “Mark scheme” only after trying.

Paper 1 style

Question 1 [3 marks] Differentiate f(x) = 3x⁴ − ⟦2|x⟧ + √{x}. [3]
Mark schemeM1 rewrite 3x⁴ − 2x⁻¹ + x^(½); A1 12x³ + ⟦2|x²⟧; A1 + ⟦1|2√{x}⟧.
Question 2 [4 marks] Find the coordinates of the point on y = x² − 6x + 10 where the gradient is 2. [4]
Mark schemeM1 dy/dx = 2x − 6; M1 2x − 6 = 2; A1 x = 4; A1 y = 16 − 24 + 10 = 2, point (4, 2).
Question 3 [3 marks] Given f(x) = 2 sin x + 3eˣ, find f′(0). [3]
Mark schemeM1 f′(x) = 2cos x + 3eˣ; M1 substitute 0; A1 2 + 3 = 5.

Paper 2 style

Question 4 [8 marks] The profit of a company is P(x) = −x² + 12x − 20 thousand dollars when x units (in hundreds) are sold. (a) Find P′(x). [2] (b) Find the rate of change of profit when x = 2. [2] (c) Find x when P′(x) = 0. [2] (d) Interpret the result in (c). [2]
Mark scheme(a) M1 differentiate; A1 −2x + 12. (b) M1 −2(2) + 12; A1 8 (thousand dollars per hundred units). (c) M1 −2x + 12 = 0; A1 x = 6. (d) R1 profit is stationary at 600 units; R1 this is the maximum profit (P″ < 0) of P(6) = 16 thousand dollars.
