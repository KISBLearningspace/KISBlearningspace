2Study Notes

an means a multiplied by itself n times.
LawRuleExample
Multiplyam × an = am+nx2 × x5 = x7
Divideam ÷ an = am−nx6 ÷ x2 = x4
Power of a power(am)n = amn(x3)4 = x12
Power of a product(ab)n = anbn(2x)3 = 8x3
Power of a quotient(⟦a|b⟧)n = ⟦an|bn⟧(⟦x|3⟧)2 = ⟦x2|9⟧
Zero, negativea0 = 1; a−n = ⟦1|an⟧x−3 = ⟦1|x3⟧
Rational (SL 1.7)a⟦1|n⟧ = ⁿ√a; a⟦m|n⟧ = (ⁿ√a)m8⟦2|3⟧ = (∛8)2 = 4
Example 1. Simplify (a) 3x2 × 4x5, (b) 6x5 ÷ 2x2, (c) (2x3)2.
  1. (a) Multiply coefficients, add powers. 3 × 4 = 12 and x2 × x5 = x2+5 = x7. Result 12x7.
  2. (b) Divide coefficients, subtract powers. 6 ÷ 2 = 3 and x5 ÷ x2 = x5−2 = x3. Result 3x3.
  3. (c) Apply the power to everything inside the bracket. (2x3)2 = 22 × (x3)2. Then 22 = 4 and (x3)2 = x3×2 = x6. Result 4x6.
Answer: (a) 12x7 (b) 3x3 (c) 4x6
Example 2. Solve 3x = 81.
  1. Write both sides with the same base. 81 = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 34.
  2. Rewrite the equation. 3x = 34.
  3. Equate the powers. When the bases are equal (and not 1), the powers must be equal, so x = 4.
Answer: x = 4
Think: Why is a0 = 1? (Hint: a3 ÷ a3.) Why must a ≠ 0?

3History & Facts

  • Nicole Oresme (about 1360): suggested powers with fractional exponents.
  • René Descartes (1637): introduced the modern way of writing powers such as x2 and x3.
  • Fact: an grows very quickly: folding paper 42 times would make a stack roughly as high as the distance to the Moon.

5Real-World Applications

  • Finance: compound interest uses A = P(1 + r)n.
  • Science: bacteria doubling, radioactive decay, earthquake and sound scales.
  • Computing: memory sizes are powers of 2 (210 = 1024).
