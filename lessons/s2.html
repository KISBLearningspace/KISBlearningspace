2Study Notes

Frequency table. Lists each value (or class interval) and its frequency f, the number of times it occurs. For continuous data we use class intervals such as 10 ≤ t < 20. The class width is the length of the interval; the midpoint is the centre of the interval.
Histogram. A bar chart for continuous data with no gaps between bars. For equal class widths the height of a bar shows the frequency. (For unequal widths the height is the frequency density = ⟦frequency|class width⟧, so that area shows frequency.)
Cumulative frequency. The running total of the frequencies up to the upper boundary of each class. A cumulative frequency graph (an ogive) plots these totals against the upper boundaries and joins the points with a smooth curve. For n values read: median at ⟦n|2⟧, lower quartile Q₁ at ⟦n|4⟧, upper quartile Q₃ at ⟦3n|4⟧, and the pth percentile at ⟦p|100⟧ × n.
Five-number summary and box plot. Minimum, Q₁, median, Q₃, maximum. Range = max − min. Interquartile range IQR = Q₃ − Q₁ (the spread of the middle 50%). A box-and-whisker diagram draws a box from Q₁ to Q₃ with a line at the median and whiskers to the smallest and largest values that are not outliers.
Outliers. A value is an outlier if it is more than 1.5 × IQR below Q₁ or above Q₃:
x < Q₁ − 1.5(IQR)   or   x > Q₃ + 1.5(IQR)
Example 1 (cumulative frequency). The times in minutes for 50 students to travel to school:
Time t (min)0–1010–2020–3030–4040–5050–60
Frequency51218942
Cumulative frequency (t < upper)51735444850
  1. Median: the middle value. n = 50, so the median is the ⟦50|2⟧ = 25th value. It lies in the class 20–30 (17 students are below 20, and 35 are below 30).
  2. Interpolate within the class. Median = 20 + ⟦25 − 17|18⟧ × 10 ≈ 24.4 min (the class width is 10 and the class frequency is 18).
  3. Lower quartile: the 12.5th value (a quarter of 50). It lies in 10–20 (5 below): Q₁ = 10 + ⟦12.5 − 5|12⟧ × 10 ≈ 16.3.
  4. Upper quartile: the 37.5th value. It lies in 30–40 (35 below): Q₃ = 30 + ⟦37.5 − 35|9⟧ × 10 ≈ 32.8.
  5. IQR = Q₃ − Q₁. 32.8 − 16.3 ≈ 16.5.
  6. 90th percentile: the 45th value. It lies in 40–50 (44 below): 40 + ⟦45 − 44|4⟧ × 10 = 42.5.
Answer: Median ≈ 24.4 min; Q₁ ≈ 16.3; Q₃ ≈ 32.8; IQR ≈ 16.5; 90th percentile ≈ 42.5 min.
Example 2 (box plot and outlier). Data (n = 12): 12, 15, 17, 18, 20, 22, 23, 25, 26, 28, 29, 60. Find the quartiles, the IQR, and test for outliers.
  1. Order the data. Already in order.
  2. Median (n = 12, average the 6th and 7th). ⟦22 + 23|2⟧ = 22.5.
  3. Q₁ = median of the lower half (12, 15, 17, 18, 20, 22). ⟦17 + 18|2⟧ = 17.5.
  4. Q₃ = median of the upper half (23, 25, 26, 28, 29, 60). ⟦26 + 28|2⟧ = 27.
  5. IQR. 27 − 17.5 = 9.5.
  6. Fences: Q₁ − 1.5 × IQR and Q₃ + 1.5 × IQR. Lower: 17.5 − 14.25 = 3.25. Upper: 27 + 14.25 = 41.25.
  7. Compare. 60 > 41.25, so 60 is an outlier. No value is below 3.25.
  8. Draw the box plot. The upper whisker stops at the largest value inside the fence, 29, and 60 is plotted as a separate point.
Answer: Q₁ = 17.5, median = 22.5, Q₃ = 27, IQR = 9.5; 60 is an outlier.
Example 3 (stem-and-leaf). Draw a stem-and-leaf diagram for: 23, 25, 31, 34, 34, 38, 41, 42, 47, 53.
  1. Choose the stems. Use the tens digits: 2, 3, 4, 5.
  2. Write each unit digit (leaf) next to its stem, in order. 23 → 2 | 3, 25 → 2 | 5, and so on.
  3. Result. 2 | 3 5   3 | 1 4 4 8   4 | 1 2 7   5 | 3
  4. Add a key. Key: 2 | 3 means 23.
  5. Read the shape. Turned on its side, it shows where the data are concentrated (here, in the 30s).
Answer: See the diagram above; the key is 2 | 3 = 23.
Example 4 (shape). Describe the shape of a histogram with a long tail to the right.
  1. Look at the tail. The long tail points to the right (high values).
  2. Name it. That is positive skew.
  3. Link to averages. The few large values pull the mean up, so mean > median.
  4. Other shapes. A long left tail is negative skew; a balanced shape is symmetric. In a box plot a longer whisker on one side shows the skew.
Answer: Positively skewed, mean > median.
Example 5 (reading a table). 18 of 50 students take 20 to 30 minutes. Find the relative frequency and say what the modal class is.
  1. Relative frequency = frequency ÷ total. ⟦18|50⟧ = 0.36.
  2. Convert to a percentage. 0.36 = 36%.
  3. Modal class. It is the class with the highest frequency: 20–30 (frequency 18).
Answer: Relative frequency 0.36 (36%); modal class 20–30.
Think: ① Why must the bars of a histogram touch, but the bars of a bar chart for categories need not? ② Why do we plot cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary? ③ If you change the class width, what happens to the shape of the histogram? (Try Lab A.)

3History & Facts

  • William Playfair (1786): invented the bar chart and line graph to show trade data, helping people see patterns in numbers.
  • Karl Pearson (1895): introduced the word “histogram”.
  • John Tukey (1977): invented the box-and-whisker plot and the stem-and-leaf plot in Exploratory Data Analysis.
  • Florence Nightingale (1850s): used coloured “rose” diagrams to show that disease killed more soldiers than battle wounds, helping to change hospital practice.

5Real-World Applications

  • Health and growth charts: percentiles show how a child compares with others of the same age.
  • Exam results: cumulative frequency finds the grade boundary at the top 25%.
  • Business: box plots compare salaries or waiting times between branches.
  • Weather: histograms of daily rainfall show how often heavy rain happens.
  • Sport: box plots compare the spread of scores of different players.

7Classwork & Homework

Classwork

  1. The ages of 9 people: 12, 14, 15, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21, 40. Find the median, Q₁, Q₃ and IQR (GDC method: exclude the median when n is odd).
    AnswerMedian 17. Lower half 12, 14, 15, 15: Q₁ = 14.5. Upper half 18, 20, 21, 40: Q₃ = 20.5. IQR = 6.
  2. Use the 1.5 × IQR rule to decide whether 40 is an outlier in CW 1.
    AnswerUpper fence = 20.5 + 1.5(6) = 29.5. 40 > 29.5, so 40 is an outlier.
  3. A cumulative frequency table for 80 marks: ≤ 20: 6, ≤ 40: 22, ≤ 60: 50, ≤ 80: 74, ≤ 100: 80. Estimate the median.
    AnswerThe median is the 40th value, in 40–60: 40 + ⟦40 − 22|28⟧ × 20 ≈ 52.9.
  4. In CW 3, estimate Q₁ and Q₃ and the IQR.
    AnswerQ₁ (20th value): 20 + ⟦20 − 6|16⟧ × 20 = 37.5. Q₃ (60th value): 60 + ⟦60 − 50|24⟧ × 20 ≈ 68.3. IQR ≈ 30.8.

Homework

  1. Draw a stem-and-leaf diagram for: 34, 41, 45, 47, 52, 52, 58, 63, 67, 71.
    Answer3 | 4; 4 | 1 5 7; 5 | 2 2 8; 6 | 3 7; 7 | 1 (key 3 | 4 means 34).
  2. Use CW 3 to estimate how many students scored more than 75.
    AnswerAt 75: 50 + ⟦75 − 60|20⟧ × 24 = 68. So 80 − 68 = 12 students.
  3. A histogram has a long tail to the right. Describe the skew and say which is larger, the mean or the median.
    AnswerPositively skewed; the mean is usually larger than the median.
  4. A frequency table has classes 0–10, 10–20, 20–30 with frequencies 6, 14, 10. Find the relative frequency of the modal class as a percentage.
    AnswerModal class 10–20: ⟦14|30⟧ ≈ 46.7%.
