2Study Notes

Zero of a function: a value of x with f(x) = 0. For a quadratic, the zeros are the roots of the equation ax² + bx + c = 0 and the x-intercepts of the graph.
Quadratic formula
x = ⟦−b ± √{b² − 4ac}|2a⟧
Discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac. If Δ > 0 there are two distinct real roots (the graph crosses the x-axis twice). If Δ = 0 there is one repeated root (the graph touches the x-axis). If Δ < 0 there are no real roots (the graph does not meet the x-axis).
Factored form
f(x) = a(x − p)(x − q)
The zeros are p and q. The axis of symmetry is x = ⟦p + q|2⟧, halfway between the zeros. The vertex has x-coordinate ⟦p + q|2⟧.
🏷️ Formula map: what each part means
Sum and product of roots
sum of the rootsp + q=−⟦b|a⟧minus b over a
Product of the roots
product of the rootspq=⟦c|a⟧c over a
Example 1 (factorising). Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
  1. One side must be 0. It is.
  2. Two numbers with product 6 and sum −5. −2 and −3.
  3. Factorise. (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0.
  4. Zero-product rule. x − 2 = 0 or x − 3 = 0.
Answer: x = 2 or x = 3
Example 2 (rearrange first). Solve 3x² = 5x + 2.
  1. Make one side zero. 3x² − 5x − 2 = 0.
  2. Factorise (a × c = −6; need sum −5, so −6 and 1). 3x² − 6x + x − 2 = 3x(x − 2) + (x − 2) = (3x + 1)(x − 2).
  3. Zero-product rule. 3x + 1 = 0 or x − 2 = 0.
Answer: x = −⟦1|3⟧ or x = 2
Example 3 (formula). Solve 2x² + 3x − 4 = 0.
  1. Identify the coefficients. a = 2, b = 3, c = −4.
  2. Discriminant. Δ = b² − 4ac = 9 − 4(2)(−4) = 9 + 32 = 41.
  3. Substitute in the formula. x = ⟦−3 ± √{41}|4⟧.
  4. Evaluate (√41 ≈ 6.403). x = ⟦−3 + 6.403|4⟧ ≈ 0.851 or x = ⟦−3 − 6.403|4⟧ ≈ −2.35.
Answer: x ≈ 0.851 or x ≈ −2.35 (3 s.f.)
Example 4 (no real roots). Show that x² + 2x + 5 has no real zeros.
  1. Calculate the discriminant. a = 1, b = 2, c = 5: Δ = 4 − 4(1)(5) = −16.
  2. Interpret. Δ < 0, so there are no real zeros.
  3. Confirm with vertex form. x² + 2x + 5 = (x + 1)² + 4. The minimum is 4 > 0, so the graph stays above the x-axis.
Answer: No real zeros.
Example 5 (unknown coefficient). For what values of k does x² + kx + 9 = 0 have exactly one root?
  1. One (repeated) root means Δ = 0. a = 1, b = k, c = 9.
  2. Write the discriminant. Δ = k² − 4(1)(9) = k² − 36.
  3. Set Δ = 0 and solve. k² = 36, so k = ±6.
Answer: k = 6 or k = −6
Example 6 (from the zeros). A parabola has zeros −1 and 5 and passes through (0, −10). Find f(x) and the vertex.
  1. Write the factorised form. f(x) = a(x + 1)(x − 5).
  2. Use (0, −10) to find a. a(1)(−5) = −10, so a = 2.
  3. Expand. f(x) = 2(x + 1)(x − 5) = 2(x² − 4x − 5) = 2x² − 8x − 10.
  4. Axis of symmetry: halfway between the zeros. x = ⟦−1 + 5|2⟧ = 2.
  5. Vertex y-value. f(2) = 2(3)(−3) = −18.
Answer: f(x) = 2x² − 8x − 10; vertex (2, −18).
Sum and product of roots (for interest, HL). If the roots are p and q then p + q = −⟦b|a⟧ and pq = ⟦c|a⟧. For x² − 7x + 10 = 0 the roots are 2 and 5: the sum is 7 and the product is 10 ✓.

Technology (GDC / Desmos)

Use the “zero” or “root” tool or the polynomial solver to check each answer. Always write the formula and the substitution in your working.

Think: ① Why must we write the equation as “… = 0” before factorising? ② Why does a negative discriminant mean the graph misses the x-axis? ③ If the roots are p and q, why is the axis of symmetry at their average?

3History & Facts

  • Brahmagupta (628 CE) and al-Khwarizmi (about 820 CE): gave general rules for solving quadratic equations in India and in Baghdad.
  • François Viète (late 1500s): discovered the link between the roots of a polynomial and its coefficients (sum and product of roots).
  • Gerolamo Cardano (1545): worked with “impossible” square roots of negative numbers, an early step toward complex numbers. This is what happens when Δ < 0.
  • Fact: the Babylonians solved quadratic problems about fields and areas on clay tablets about 4000 years ago, without using a formula.

5Real-World Applications

  • Projectile landing: the positive root of the height equation is the time the ball hits the ground.
  • Business break-even: profit equals zero at the zeros of the profit quadratic.
  • Bridges and arches: the zeros show where the arch meets the ground, which gives its span.
  • Safety: a negative discriminant tells an engineer that two paths or curves never meet.
  • Design: sizing a rectangular garden or pool so that its area is exactly a target value.

Classwork

  1. Solve x² − 7x + 12 = 0.
    Answer
    (x − 3)(x − 4) = 0
    so x = 3 or x = 4
  2. Solve 3x² = 5x + 2.
    Answer
    3x² − 5x − 2 = 0
    (3x + 1)(x − 2) = 0
    x = −⟦1|3⟧ or x = 2
  3. How many real solutions does x² − 6x + 10 = 0 have?
    Answer
    Δ = 36 − 40
    = −4 < 0
    so there are none
  4. A parabola has zeros −2 and 4 and passes through (0, −16). Find its equation.
    Answer
    f(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 4)
    f(0) = −8a = −16
    so a = 2: f(x) = 2(x + 2)(x − 4)
